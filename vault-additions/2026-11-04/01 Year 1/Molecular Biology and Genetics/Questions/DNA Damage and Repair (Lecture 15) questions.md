# DNA Damage and Repair (Lecture 15): questions

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Damage and Repair (Lecture 15)]]

## 27 questions

### Causes of DNA damage

> [!question]- What is the difference between endogenous and exogenous DNA damage? Give two examples of each.
> Endogenous damage comes from inside the cell (oxygen free radicals, replication errors). Exogenous damage comes from outside (ionising radiation, UV, environmental chemicals).

> [!question]- Which oxygen radical is the most reactive in biological systems?
> The hydroxyl radical, HO·.

### Oxidative damage

> [!question]- What chemical groups do oxygen radicals add to DNA bases?
> Hydroxyl (–OH) and carbonyl (C=O) groups, e.g. thymine glycol, 8-oxo-dG.

> [!question]- What is depurination?
> Breaking of the glycosidic bond between the sugar and a purine base (A or G), leaving a sugar with no base.

> [!question]- What does deamination of cytosine produce, and why is it a problem?
> Uracil. Uracil pairs with adenine, so after replication a C–G pair can become T–A, a mutation.

> [!question]- Why is a double strand break more dangerous than a single strand break?
> Both backbones are broken, so there is no intact complementary strand left to use as a template for repair.

### UV damage and replication errors

> [!question]- What kind of damage does UV light cause in DNA?
> It crosslinks adjacent pyrimidines on the same strand into pyrimidine dimers (cyclobutane dimers and 6-4 photoproducts).

> [!question]- What are the two types of UV-induced pyrimidine dimer?
> The cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer (C5–C5 and C6–C6 bonds) and the 6-4 photoproduct (C6 to C4 bond).

> [!question]- What does a thymine dimer do to the shape of the double helix?
> It kinks and distorts it, blocking replication and transcription.

> [!question]- Name three types of replication error.
> Mismatches, insertions and deletions (insertions and deletions arise from slipped DNA); also stalled replication forks.

### Major types of DNA repair

> [!question]- Name the three groups of DNA repair and one pathway in each.
> Direct reversal (photoreactivation); excision repair (BER, NER, MMR); double-strand break repair (HR, NHEJ, MMEJ).

> [!question]- What template is used in excision repair?
> The undamaged complementary strand.

### Direct reversal by photoreactivation

> [!question]- What single protein can repair thymine dimers?
> Photolyase (photoreactivation).

> [!question]- What two cofactors does photolyase use, and what does each do?
> 5,10-MTHF absorbs a photon of blue light and passes the energy on; FADH⁻ transfers an electron to the dimer to split it.

> [!question]- Why can humans not repair thymine dimers by photoreactivation?
> Placental mammals do not have photolyase, so they use nucleotide excision repair instead.

### How nucleotide excision repair works

> [!question]- What multi-protein process can repair thymine dimers?
> Nucleotide excision repair (NER).

> [!question]- Outline the four steps of nucleotide excision repair.
> An excinuclease cuts either side of the lesion; DNA helicase removes the damaged oligonucleotide; DNA polymerase fills the gap using the undamaged strand; DNA ligase seals the nick.

> [!question]- How long is the piece removed by NER in E. coli and in humans?
> 13 nucleotides in E. coli, 29 nucleotides in humans.

> [!question]- Which DNA polymerase fills the gap in NER in E. coli and in humans?
> DNA polymerase I in E. coli, DNA polymerase ε in humans.

### How mismatch repair works

> [!question]- How does mismatch repair tell the new strand from the template strand in E. coli?
> The template strand is methylated at GATC sites but the new strand is not methylated yet (hemimethylated DNA), so the unmethylated strand is the new one.

> [!question]- What do MutS, MutL and MutH each do?
> MutS binds the mismatch; MutL binds MutS and the complex threads the DNA; MutH binds a hemimethylated GATC and cuts the unmethylated strand.

> [!question]- Where does MutH cut?
> The unmethylated (new) strand, 5′ of the G in the GATC sequence.

> [!question]- Why must mismatch repair happen soon after replication?
> After a few minutes Dam methylase methylates the new strand too, and the strands can no longer be distinguished.

### Diseases from defective DNA repair

> [!question]- Which repair pathway is defective in xeroderma pigmentosum, and what is the result?
> Nucleotide excision repair; UV-induced skin cancer.

> [!question]- Which repair pathway is defective in HNPCC?
> Mismatch repair; it causes colorectal cancer.

> [!question]- Which repair pathway do BRCA1 and BRCA2 work in?
> Homologous recombination (double strand break repair); defects predispose to breast and ovarian cancer.

> [!question]- Name two NER defect syndromes with no cancer predisposition.
> Cockayne syndrome and trichothiodystrophy (photosensitivity, physical and mental retardation).
