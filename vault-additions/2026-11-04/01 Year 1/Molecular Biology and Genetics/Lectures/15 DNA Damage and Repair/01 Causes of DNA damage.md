# Causes of DNA damage

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Damage and Repair (Lecture 15)]]

*Source: Lecture 15: DNA damage and repair (PowerPoint), slides 2-5*

## Learning outcomes
- The causes and types of DNA damage
- The principles of some major DNA repair mechanisms: photoreactivation, mismatch repair and nucleotide excision repair
- The diseases that result from defective human DNA repair

## Two sources of damage

| Source | Where it comes from | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[[Endogenous DNA damage|Endogenous]]** | inside the cell | chemicals made in the cell, such as **oxygen free radicals**; errors during DNA metabolism, especially **replication** |
| **[[Exogenous DNA damage|Exogenous]]** | outside the cell | **ionising radiation**, **UV radiation**, chemicals in the environment |

- The **hydroxyl radical (HO·)** is by far the most reactive oxygen radical in biological systems

> [!quote] Alberts 7e, p. 286
> The DNA of each human cell loses about 18,000 purine bases (adenine and guanine) every day because their N-glycosyl linkages to deoxyribose break, a spontaneous hydrolysis reaction called depurination.

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the difference between endogenous and exogenous DNA damage? Give two examples of each.
> Endogenous damage comes from inside the cell (oxygen free radicals, replication errors). Exogenous damage comes from outside (ionising radiation, UV, environmental chemicals).

> [!question]- Which oxygen radical is the most reactive in biological systems?
> The hydroxyl radical, HO·.
