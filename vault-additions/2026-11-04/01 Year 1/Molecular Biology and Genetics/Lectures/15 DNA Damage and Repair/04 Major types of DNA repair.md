# Major types of DNA repair

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Damage and Repair (Lecture 15)]]

*Source: Lecture 15: DNA damage and repair (PowerPoint), slides 13*

Different repair pathways have evolved to maintain **genomic stability**:

| Group | Pathway | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct reversal** | **[[Photoreactivation]] (PR)** | undoes the damage itself, no nucleotides removed |
| **Excision repair** | **[[Base excision repair]] (BER)** | removes a single damaged base |
| | **[[Nucleotide excision repair]] (NER)** | removes a stretch of nucleotides around a bulky lesion |
| | **[[Mismatch repair]] (MMR)** | removes a stretch of the new strand containing a mismatch |
| **Double-strand break repair** | **[[Homologous recombination]] (HR)** | uses the sister chromatid as a template, accurate |
| | **[[Non-homologous end joining]] (NHEJ)** | joins the broken ends directly, can lose bases |
| | **Microhomology-mediated end joining (MMEJ)** | joins ends using short matching sequences |

- In **excision repair**, the damaged part is cut out and replaced by copying the **undamaged strand** as a template

## Questions
> [!question]- Name the three groups of DNA repair and one pathway in each.
> Direct reversal (photoreactivation); excision repair (BER, NER, MMR); double-strand break repair (HR, NHEJ, MMEJ).

> [!question]- What template is used in excision repair?
> The undamaged complementary strand.
