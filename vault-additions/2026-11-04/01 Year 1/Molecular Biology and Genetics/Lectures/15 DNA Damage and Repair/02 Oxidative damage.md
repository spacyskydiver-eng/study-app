# Oxidative damage

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Damage and Repair (Lecture 15)]]

*Source: Lecture 15: DNA damage and repair (PowerPoint), slides 6-8*

## Base modifications
- Oxygen radicals add **hydroxyl (–OH)** and **carbonyl (C=O)** groups to the bases
- Examples: **5-hydroxy-dU**, **5-hydroxy-dC**, **[[Thymine glycol|thymine glycol]]**, uracil glycol, **[[8-oxoguanine|8-oxo-dG]]**, 8-oxo-dA, Fapy-dA and Fapy-dG
- The **C4–C5 double bond of pyrimidines** is particularly sensitive to attack by the hydroxyl radical

## Depurination and deamination
- **[[Depurination]]**: the **glycosidic bond** between the sugar and a purine (G or A) is broken by water, leaving a **depurinated sugar** with no base
- **[[Deamination]]**: an **amino group** is removed. **Cytosine becomes uracil**, which pairs with A, so a C–G pair can become T–A after replication

## Strand breaks
- **[[Single strand break]] (SSB)**: one backbone is broken
- **[[Double strand break]] (DSB)**: both backbones are broken at nearby points, the most dangerous kind because there is no intact strand to copy

> [!quote] Alberts 7e, p. 287
> Depurination and deamination are the most frequent spontaneous chemical reactions known to create serious DNA damage in cells.

## Questions
> [!question]- What chemical groups do oxygen radicals add to DNA bases?
> Hydroxyl (–OH) and carbonyl (C=O) groups, e.g. thymine glycol, 8-oxo-dG.

> [!question]- What is depurination?
> Breaking of the glycosidic bond between the sugar and a purine base (A or G), leaving a sugar with no base.

> [!question]- What does deamination of cytosine produce, and why is it a problem?
> Uracil. Uracil pairs with adenine, so after replication a C–G pair can become T–A, a mutation.

> [!question]- Why is a double strand break more dangerous than a single strand break?
> Both backbones are broken, so there is no intact complementary strand left to use as a template for repair.
