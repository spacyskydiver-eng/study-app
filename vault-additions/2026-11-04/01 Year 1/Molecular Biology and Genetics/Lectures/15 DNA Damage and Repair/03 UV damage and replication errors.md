# UV damage and replication errors

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Damage and Repair (Lecture 15)]]

*Source: Lecture 15: DNA damage and repair (PowerPoint), slides 9-11*

## UV damage
- **UV light** crosslinks **adjacent pyrimidines on the same strand** (usually two thymines) to form a **[[Pyrimidine dimer|pyrimidine dimer]]**
- Two forms:
  - **[[Cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer|Cyclobutane thymine dimer]]**: two bonds join C5–C5 and C6–C6, making a four-membered ring
  - **[[6-4 photoproduct]]**: one bond from C6 of one pyrimidine to C4 of the next
- The dimer **kinks** the double helix and distorts it, which blocks replication and transcription

## Replication errors
- **Mismatch**: the wrong base is put in, e.g. A opposite A
- **Insertion**: an extra base on the new strand loops out ("slipped DNA")
- **Deletion**: a base on the template loops out, so the new strand misses one ("slipped DNA")
- **Stalled replication fork**: the fork stops at damage it cannot copy past

## Questions
> [!question]- What kind of damage does UV light cause in DNA?
> It crosslinks adjacent pyrimidines on the same strand into pyrimidine dimers (cyclobutane dimers and 6-4 photoproducts).

> [!question]- What are the two types of UV-induced pyrimidine dimer?
> The cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer (C5–C5 and C6–C6 bonds) and the 6-4 photoproduct (C6 to C4 bond).

> [!question]- What does a thymine dimer do to the shape of the double helix?
> It kinks and distorts it, blocking replication and transcription.

> [!question]- Name three types of replication error.
> Mismatches, insertions and deletions (insertions and deletions arise from slipped DNA); also stalled replication forks.
