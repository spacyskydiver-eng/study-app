# Why DNA sequencing matters

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Sequencing (Lecture 11)]]

*Source: Lecture 11: DNA Sequencing, Prof Richard Bowater (PowerPoint), slides 7-10*

## Learning outcomes
- Sequencing of large genomes became possible with improved **biological and computational** resources
- The historical context of the most significant developments in sequencing, especially for genomes
- How further advances keep **increasing speed** and **reducing the size and cost** of sequencing

## Where this lecture fits
The DNA manipulation lectures in BIO-4018A cover:
1. **Recombinant DNA**: techniques used in molecular biology and genetic engineering ([[Plasmids and Recombinant DNA (Lecture 8)|Lecture 8]])
2. **Polymerase chain reaction**: its uses in molecular biology and genetic engineering ([[Polymerase Chain Reaction (Lecture 10)|Lecture 10]])
3. **DNA sequencing**: techniques that generate lots of sequence information about DNA molecules (this lecture)

- A short video on Blackboard, "**DNA Sequencing Background**", gives a fuller description of how the methods developed

## From the 2017 Nature review

> [!quote] Shendure et al. (2017) Nature
> We predict that in the long view of history, the impact of DNA sequencing will be on a par with that of the microscope.
