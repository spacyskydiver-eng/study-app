# The Human Genome Project

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[DNA Sequencing (Lecture 11)]]

*Source: Lecture 11: DNA Sequencing, Prof Richard Bowater (PowerPoint), slides 13*

- In **2001** two **first draft** human genome sequences were published (Nature, 15 Feb 2001; Science, 16 Feb 2001)

| | Public project ([[Human Genome Project]]) | Celera Genomics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Led by | **Francis Collins** | **J. Craig Venter** |
| Strategy | **hierarchical (BAC-by-BAC) shotgun**: a minimal set of overlapping BACs selected from a map, each shotgun sequenced | **[[Whole genome shotgun sequencing|whole-genome shotgun]]**: the whole genome broken into random pieces, sequenced and assembled |
| Published | Nature | Science |

- The finished human sequence followed in **2004**
- A Blackboard video gives a fuller description

> [!quote] Shendure et al. (2017) Nature
> For the 'hierarchical shotgun' strategy that emerged as the workhorse of the Human Genome Project (HGP), large fragments of the human genome were cloned into bacterial artificial chromosomes (BACs). DNA from each BAC was fragmented, size-selected and sub-cloned.

> [!quote] Shendure et al. (2017) Nature
> The HGP's human genome ... came first as a draft (2001) and then as a finished sequence (2004).

## Questions
> [!question]- When were the first draft human genome sequences published, and by whom?
> 2001: the public Human Genome Project (Francis Collins) and Celera Genomics (J. Craig Venter).

> [!question]- How did the strategies of the public Human Genome Project and Celera differ?
> The public project used hierarchical (BAC-by-BAC) shotgun sequencing of overlapping BACs from a map; Celera used whole-genome shotgun sequencing of random fragments of the whole genome.

> [!question]- When was the finished human genome sequence completed?
> 2004 (the draft was 2001).
