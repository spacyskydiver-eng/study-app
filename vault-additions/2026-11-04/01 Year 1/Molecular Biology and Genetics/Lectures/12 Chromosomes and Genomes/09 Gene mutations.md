# Gene mutations

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 43, 45-46*

## Point mutations
- **[[Transition mutation|Transition]]**: pyrimidine → pyrimidine (C↔T) or purine → purine (A↔G)
- **[[Transversion mutation|Transversion]]**: pyrimidine ↔ purine (C/T ↔ A/G)

## Effect on the protein
- **[[Missense mutation]]**: a different amino acid, e.g. AAG (K) → AGG (R)
- **[[Nonsense mutation]]**: a stop codon, e.g. AAG (K) → TAG (stop)
- **[[Frameshift mutation]]**: an insertion or deletion shifts the reading frame and usually introduces a **stop codon 3′ to the mutation**, e.g.

| | Sequence | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| original | CAG GGC AAG GAT AGA | Q G K D R |
| one base inserted | CAG GGA CAA GGA TAG | Q G Q G stop |

## Mutations make new alleles
- **Most often, loss-of-function alleles result**
  - E.g. pea **Le** encodes **[[Gibberellin 3β-hydroxylase|gibberellin 3β-hydroxylase]]**. The mutant **le** has an **A → G transition** changing **Ala229 → Thr (A229T)**, leaving very little GA hydroxylase activity (dwarf peas, Mendel's tall/short)
- **Gain-of-function alleles**, although rare, are possible
  - E.g. **Ras G12V**: normally Ras is active when bound to GTP and switches itself off by its **GTPase activity**. G12V **lacks GTPase activity**, so it is **locked in the active GTP-bound form**, driving **uncontrolled cell division**: it is **oncogenic**

## Questions
> [!question]- Distinguish a transition from a transversion.
> A transition swaps a purine for a purine or a pyrimidine for a pyrimidine (A↔G, C↔T); a transversion swaps a purine for a pyrimidine or vice versa.

> [!question]- Distinguish missense and nonsense mutations, with an example of each.
> Missense changes one amino acid, e.g. AAG (Lys) → AGG (Arg); nonsense creates a stop codon, e.g. AAG (Lys) → TAG (stop).

> [!question]- Why do frameshift mutations usually make a truncated protein?
> Shifting the reading frame changes every codon after the mutation and usually soon introduces a stop codon 3′ to the mutation.

> [!question]- What mutation causes the pea le allele and what is its effect?
> An A → G transition changing Ala229 to Thr (A229T) in gibberellin 3β-hydroxylase, leaving very little enzyme activity: a loss-of-function allele.

> [!question]- Explain why Ras G12V is oncogenic.
> It lacks GTPase activity, so it cannot hydrolyse GTP and stays locked in the active form, continuously signalling cell division: a gain-of-function mutation.

> [!question]- Which kind of new allele do mutations most often produce?
> Loss-of-function alleles; gain-of-function alleles are rare.
