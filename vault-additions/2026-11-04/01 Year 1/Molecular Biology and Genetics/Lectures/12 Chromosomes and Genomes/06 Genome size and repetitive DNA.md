# Genome size and repetitive DNA

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 31-33*

## [[C value paradox]]
- **C value**: the amount of DNA in the **haploid nuclear genome**
- Genome size **does not track organism complexity** or gene number in eukaryotes: e.g. wheat (1.7 × 10¹⁰ bp) has about 5 times the DNA of humans, and some amphibians and flowering plants have enormous genomes
- Humans have about **20,000 genes**; in humans **functional genes make up about 2%** of the genome
- The extra DNA is mainly **repetitive DNA**

## Repetitive elements

| Class | Type | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highly repetitive | **[[Satellite DNA|satellite DNA]]** | non-coding tandem repeats, the main component of **centromeres** and other heterochromatin |
| Middle repetitive, tandem | **multiple copy genes** | e.g. **rRNA genes** (28S, 18S, 5.8S cluster repeated hundreds of times) |
| | **minisatellites** | **[[Variable number tandem repeat|VNTRs]]**: 1 to 5 kb blocks of 15 to 100 bp units |
| | **microsatellites** | **[[Short tandem repeat|STRs]]**: 2 to 5 bp motifs repeated 5 to 50 times; most common in humans is **(CA)n** |
| Middle repetitive, interspersed (**[[Retrotransposon|retrotransposons]]**) | **SINEs** | short (< 500 bp), e.g. **Alu** elements (~300 bp, over 1 million copies, over 10% of the human genome) |
| | **LINEs** | long, e.g. **L1** elements (6.4 kb, 40,000 copies per haploid human genome) |

- 10 families of retrotransposons make up **over 50% of maize** nuclear DNA

> [!quote] Alberts 7e, p. 314
> Together the LINEs and SINEs make up more than 30% of the human genome.

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the C value, and what is the C value paradox?
> The C value is the DNA content of the haploid nuclear genome; the paradox is that it does not correlate with organism complexity or gene number in eukaryotes.

> [!question]- Roughly what percentage of the human genome is functional genes?
> About 2%.

> [!question]- What is satellite DNA, and where is it found?
> Highly repetitive non-coding tandem repeats; the main component of centromeres and other heterochromatin.

> [!question]- Distinguish minisatellites from microsatellites.
> Minisatellites (VNTRs) are 1–5 kb blocks of 15–100 bp repeats; microsatellites (STRs) are 2–5 bp motifs repeated 5–50 times, such as (CA)n.

> [!question]- What are SINEs and LINEs? Give an example of each.
> Interspersed retrotransposons: SINEs are short (< 500 bp), e.g. Alu (~300 bp, >1 million copies); LINEs are long, e.g. L1 (6.4 kb, ~40,000 copies).

> [!question]- Why are rRNA genes present in many copies?
> Cells need large amounts of rRNA, so the 28S/18S/5.8S gene cluster is repeated in tandem hundreds of times.
