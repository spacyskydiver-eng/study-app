# Eukaryotic chromosomes

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 22-26*

## Where the DNA is
- **Linear nuclear chromosomes**, each with a **[[Centromere|centromere]]**, a short **p arm**, a long **q arm** and a **[[Telomere|telomere]]** at each end
- **Circular mitochondrial** (and plastid) chromosomes

## Packaging

| Level | Diameter |
| --- | --- |
| DNA double helix | 2 nm |
| **[[Nucleosome|Nucleosomes]]** ("beads on a string") | 11 nm |
| **Solenoid** (30 nm fibre), with **H1 histone** | 30 nm |
| **Chromatin fibre** in **looped domains** | 300 nm |
| **Chromatid** | 700 nm |
| **Metaphase chromosome** | 1400 nm |

- A nucleosome is a **[[Histone|histone]] octamer**, **2 copies each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4**, with **147 bp** of DNA wrapped around it
- **Linker (spacer) DNA plus histone H1** joins neighbouring nucleosomes

## Centromere position

| Centromere location | Name |
| --- | --- |
| middle | **metacentric** |
| between middle and end | **submetacentric** |
| close to the end | **acrocentric** |
| at the end | **telocentric** |

## Chromosomes are linear arrays of genes
- E.g. *Arabidopsis thaliana*: genome of **5 chromosomes**, **120 Mb**, about **27,000 genes**, an average of **one gene every 4.4 kb**

## Questions
> [!question]- What is a nucleosome made of?
> A histone octamer (two each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4) with 147 bp of DNA wrapped around it.

> [!question]- What does histone H1 do?
> It binds the linker DNA between nucleosomes and helps pack them into the 30 nm solenoid fibre.

> [!question]- List the levels of DNA packaging from the double helix to the metaphase chromosome.
> DNA (2 nm) → nucleosomes (11 nm) → solenoid/30 nm fibre → looped chromatin fibre (300 nm) → chromatid (700 nm) → metaphase chromosome (1400 nm).

> [!question]- Name the four chromosome types based on centromere position.
> Metacentric (middle), submetacentric (between middle and end), acrocentric (close to end), telocentric (at the end).

> [!question]- What are the p and q arms of a chromosome?
> The short (p) and long (q) arms on either side of the centromere.

> [!question]- Where is DNA found in a eukaryotic cell?
> In linear chromosomes in the nucleus and in circular chromosomes in mitochondria (and plastids).
