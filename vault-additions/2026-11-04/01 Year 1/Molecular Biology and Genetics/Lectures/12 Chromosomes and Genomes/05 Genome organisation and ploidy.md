# Genome organisation and ploidy

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 28-30, 34*

- Eukaryotes have **multiple different chromosomes**: the **[[Karyotype|karyotype]]** shows them all (human male: 22 pairs plus X and Y)
- Human chromosome 1 is the largest (**249 million bp**), chromosome 2 next (**243 million bp**). **Chromosome 2 is a fusion of two ape chromosomes**
- Humans have two types of chromosome:
  - **[[Autosome|Autosomes]]**: the matching pairs, the same in both sexes
  - **[[Sex chromosome|Sex-determining chromosomes]]**: X and Y
- Both separate into different cells at the **first meiotic (reductional) division**

## Haploid number
- **[[Haploid number]] (n)** = the number of different chromosomes (the different autosomes plus half the sex chromosomes) = **half the diploid number (2n)**

| Species | DNA content (bp) | Haploid number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yeast (*S. cerevisiae*) | 1.2 × 10⁷ | 16 |
| Fruit fly (*D. melanogaster*) | 1.4 × 10⁸ | 4 |
| Chicken | 1.2 × 10⁹ | 39 |
| Mouse | 2.7 × 10⁹ | 20 |
| **Human** | **3.3 × 10⁹** | **23** |
| Wheat (*T. aestivum*) | 1.7 × 10¹⁰ | 21 |

## Ploidy

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **[[Aneuploidy]]** | **2n plus or minus** whole chromosomes |
| Monosomy | 2n − 1 |
| Trisomy | 2n + 1 |
| Tetrasomy, pentasomy | 2n + 2, 2n + 3 |
| **[[Euploidy]]** | **multiples of n** |
| Diploidy | 2n |
| **[[Polyploidy]]** | 3n, 4n, 5n … (triploidy 3n, tetraploidy 4n …) |
| **Autopolyploidy** | multiples of the **same** genome |
| **Allopolyploidy** (amphidiploidy) | multiples of **different** genomes |

- Example of allopolyploidy: *Brassica napus* (rapeseed, AACC, n = 19) arose from *B. rapa* (AA, n = 10) and *B. oleracea* (CC, n = 9)

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the difference between autosomes and sex chromosomes?
> Autosomes are the chromosome pairs that are the same in both sexes; sex chromosomes (X and Y in humans) determine sex.

> [!question]- Define the haploid number (n).
> The number of different chromosomes: the different autosomes plus half the sex chromosomes; half the diploid number.

> [!question]- What is the human haploid number and genome size?
> n = 23; about 3.3 × 10⁹ bp.

> [!question]- Distinguish aneuploidy from euploidy.
> Aneuploidy is 2n plus or minus whole chromosomes (e.g. trisomy 2n + 1); euploidy is whole multiples of the haploid set (2n, 3n, 4n).

> [!question]- Distinguish autopolyploidy from allopolyploidy.
> Autopolyploidy: multiple copies of the same genome; allopolyploidy (amphidiploidy): multiple copies of different genomes, e.g. Brassica napus from B. rapa and B. oleracea.

> [!question]- What is unusual about human chromosome 2?
> It formed by the fusion of two ape chromosomes.
