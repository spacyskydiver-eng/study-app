# Dosage compensation and X inactivation

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 40-41*

- **[[Dosage compensation]]** in humans happens by **[[X inactivation|X inactivation]]**: in females, **one X chromosome is transcriptionally inactive**, condensed into a **[[Barr body]]**
- Number of Barr bodies = **number of X chromosomes − 1** (e.g. 46,XY and 45,X: 0; 46,XX and 47,XXY: 1; 47,XXX: 2)
- Barr bodies can be stained with **methylene blue**

## How inactivation works
- It needs the **XIC** (**X-inactivation centre**): about **1 Mbp**, **4 genes**
- The best-studied gene is **[[XIST]]** (**X inactivation specific transcript**)
  - XIST is **expressed on the inactivated chromosome**
  - XIST **lacks an ORF**: its RNA is not translated but **binds (coats) the chromosome** that made it
  - In mice, **Xist deletions abolish X inactivation**

## The Lyon hypothesis
- X inactivation happens **early in embryo development**, and **which X is inactivated is random** (the **[[Lyon hypothesis]]**)
- The choice is kept in all daughter cells, so **mammalian females are mosaics**
- E.g. **calico and tortoiseshell cats**: the orange and black coat-colour alleles are on the X, so patches show which X is active

> [!quote] Klug 12e, p. 442
> The Xist lncRNA is expressed on the inactivated X chromosome and coats the entire chromosome, converting it into a Barr body.

## Questions
> [!question]- What is dosage compensation, and how is it achieved in humans?
> Equalising X-linked gene expression between XX females and XY males; one X in each female cell is inactivated.

> [!question]- How many Barr bodies are there in cells of a 47, XXY individual and of a 47, XXX individual?
> 47, XXY: one; 47, XXX: two (number of X chromosomes minus one).

> [!question]- What is XIST, and how does it inactivate an X chromosome?
> X inactivation specific transcript, a gene in the X-inactivation centre; its RNA has no ORF and coats the chromosome it is made from, silencing it.

> [!question]- State the Lyon hypothesis.
> X inactivation happens early in development and which X is inactivated in each cell is random, so females are mosaics of cells expressing different X chromosomes.

> [!question]- Why are calico cats almost always female?
> The coat-colour alleles are on the X; a female heterozygote has patches where different Xs are inactivated, giving the mosaic pattern; males have only one X.
