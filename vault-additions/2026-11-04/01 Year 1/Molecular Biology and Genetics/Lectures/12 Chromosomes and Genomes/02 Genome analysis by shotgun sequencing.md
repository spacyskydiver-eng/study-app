# Genome analysis by shotgun sequencing

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 12-14*

- **[[Whole genome shotgun sequencing|Whole genome sequencing (WGS)]]** = **shotgun sequencing**, now the most widely used method for sequencing genomes
- Genomic DNA is cut into **multiple overlapping fragments** (e.g. by digestion with different restriction enzymes), which are sequenced
- Overlapping sequences are **aligned by computer programs** to build a series of contiguous fragments, or **[[Contig|contigs]]**, and contigs are joined to assemble a whole chromosome

## Contig building
1. **Compare** sequences: find reads with identical overlapping sequence
2. **Join** sequences: merge them into a consensus
3. **Edit**: check and correct differences

- See [[DNA Sequencing (Lecture 11)|Lecture 11]] for how the reads themselves are generated

## Questions
> [!question]- What is whole genome (shotgun) sequencing?
> The genome is broken into many random overlapping fragments, each is sequenced, and computer programs assemble them by their overlaps into contigs and whole chromosomes.

> [!question]- What is a contig?
> A continuous stretch of sequence assembled from overlapping sequenced fragments.

> [!question]- What are the three steps of contig building?
> Compare sequences, join sequences, edit.
