# Human sex determination

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 36-39*

- **XX** typically female, **XY** typically male
- Genetic syndromes show that **the presence of a Y chromosome is the determining factor**:
  - **[[Klinefelter syndrome]] (47, XXY)**: male
  - **[[Turner syndrome]] (45, X)**: female

## SRY
- The region of the Y involved is **[[SRY]]** (**sex-determining region Y**)
- SRY was first identified in **XY females** whose Y was **deleted for SRY**
- **XX mice transgenic for Sry** develop as **males**
- SRY is expressed from **6 to 8 weeks** of embryo development and encodes **testis-determining factor (TDF)**, which makes the undifferentiated gonads become **testes**

## The Y chromosome
- At least **75 genes** (the X has **900 to 1400**); Y is **59 Mbp**, X is **156 Mbp**
- Most of the Y is **male specific**; the **pseudoautosomal regions (PARs)** at its tips match the X and pair with it in meiosis
- **Euchromatin**: less condensed, contains most genes, transcriptionally active. **Heterochromatin**: densely packed, genes suppressed

## Sex as a spectrum
- Ainsworth (2015) "Sex redefined": between typical male and typical female there are many variations (subtle, moderate, 46,XY DSD, ovotesticular DSD, 46,XX testicular DSD), e.g. XX males usually carry **SRY**

> [!quote] Klug 12e, p. 156
> At six to eight weeks of development, the SRY gene becomes active in XY embryos. SRY encodes a protein that causes the undifferentiated gonadal tissue of the embryo to form testes.

## Questions
> [!question]- How do Klinefelter and Turner syndromes show that the Y chromosome determines sex?
> Klinefelter (47, XXY) individuals are male despite two X chromosomes; Turner (45, X) individuals are female with no Y; so the presence of a Y determines maleness.

> [!question]- What does SRY stand for, and what does it encode?
> Sex-determining region Y; it encodes testis-determining factor (TDF).

> [!question]- Give two pieces of evidence that SRY is the male-determining gene.
> XY females have a Y chromosome missing SRY; XX mice given an Sry transgene develop as males.

> [!question]- When is SRY expressed and what does it cause?
> From 6 to 8 weeks of embryo development; undifferentiated gonads become testes.

> [!question]- What are the pseudoautosomal regions?
> Regions at the ends of the Y chromosome that are homologous to the X and pair with it in meiosis.

> [!question]- Distinguish euchromatin from heterochromatin.
> Euchromatin is less condensed, contains most genes and is transcribed; heterochromatin is densely packed and its genes are suppressed.
