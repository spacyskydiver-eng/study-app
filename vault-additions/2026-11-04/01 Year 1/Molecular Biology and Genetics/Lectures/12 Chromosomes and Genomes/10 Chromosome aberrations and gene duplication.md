# Chromosome aberrations and gene duplication

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 44, 47-48*

## Chromosome aberrations
- **[[Chromosomal duplication|Duplication]]**: a segment is present twice, e.g. after **unequal crossing over** between misaligned homologues (one chromatid gains a copy, the other loses one)
- **[[Chromosomal inversion|Inversion]]**: a chromosome **loops**, **breaks** at two points and the segment **rejoins the wrong way round**, e.g. ABCDEFGH → ABFEDCGH

## Why gene duplication matters
- After a gene is **duplicated**, one copy keeps the original function while the other is free to mutate and **evolve a new function** (A → A A → A B)
- The **globin family**: from one ancestral gene (700 to 800 million years ago), duplications gave **myoglobin** (chromosome 22), the **α-globin** cluster (chromosome 16) and the **β-globin** cluster (chromosome 11: ε, Gγ, Aγ, δ, β)
- **Adult haemoglobin** is a tetramer of **2 α + 2 β**; **fetal haemoglobin** is **2 α + 2 γ**

## Questions
> [!question]- How can unequal crossing over cause a duplication?
> If homologous chromosomes are misaligned when they cross over, one product gets an extra copy of a segment (duplication) and the other loses it (deletion).

> [!question]- How does a chromosomal inversion form?
> A chromosome loops, breaks at two points and the segment between rejoins in the reverse orientation.

> [!question]- Why is gene duplication important in evolution?
> One copy keeps the original function while the other can accumulate mutations and evolve a new function.

> [!question]- Give an example of a gene family that arose by duplication, and how its products differ.
> The globins: myoglobin, α-globins and β-globins (ε, γ, δ, β); adult haemoglobin is 2α2β, fetal haemoglobin is 2α2γ.
