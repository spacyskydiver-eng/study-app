# Learning outcomes and genetic material

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Genomes (Lectures 12 to 14)]]

*Source: Lectures 12, 13, 14: Chromosomes and Genomes, Parts 1 to 3 (PowerPoint), slides 4-10*

## Learning outcomes
- The structure of **prokaryotic and eukaryotic chromosomes**
- The structure of **eukaryotic genomes**, including **ploidy**, **autosomes** and **sex-determining chromosomes**
- **Sex determination** in humans
- **Dosage compensation** in humans
- **Mutation** and **genetic variation**

## Genetics
- **Genetics** is the branch of biology concerned with **heredity and variation**

## The genetic material
- Viruses are classified by their genome:

| Virus genome | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **ssDNA** | single-stranded DNA |
| **dsDNA** | double-stranded DNA |
| **ssRNA**, positive sense | can be translated directly |
| **ssRNA**, negative sense | must first be copied; the copy is positive sense and can be translated |
| **dsRNA** | double-stranded RNA |

- **Tobacco mosaic virus** is an example of an RNA virus (rod-shaped)
- For **all other organisms, DNA is the genetic material**
- Recap: the **Avery, MacLeod and McCarty** experiment ([[Heredity and DNA|Lecture 1]]): an extract of IIIS cells transformed IIR cells unless treated with **DNase**; RNase and protease had no effect, so the active factor is **DNA**

## Questions
> [!question]- What is genetics?
> The branch of biology concerned with heredity and variation.

> [!question]- Name the four types of viral genome.
> ssDNA, dsDNA, ssRNA (positive or negative sense) and dsRNA.

> [!question]- What is the difference between positive-sense and negative-sense ssRNA?
> Positive-sense RNA can be translated directly; negative-sense RNA must first be copied, and the complementary (positive-sense) copy is translated.

> [!question]- In the Avery, MacLeod and McCarty experiment, which treatment stopped transformation, and what did that show?
> Treating the IIIS extract with DNase (deoxyribonuclease); so the transforming factor is DNA.
