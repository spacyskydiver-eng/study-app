# Factorising when the subject appears twice

> [!path] [[Professional Skills for Biosciences]] › [[Rearranging Equations (Maths 1)]]

*Source: Mathematical Concepts: Rearranging Equations, Dr Beth Williams (PowerPoint) and Maths Basic Rules helpsheet, slides 16-18*

- If the letter you want appears more than once, **collect** all those terms on one side and **factorise**
- a(x + y) = b(x + y): ax + ay = bx + by → ax − bx = by − ay → x(a − b) = −y(a − b) → **x = −y** (a ≠ b)
- ax + b(x − a) = ay → ax + bx = ay + ab → x(a + b) = a(y + b) → **x = a(y + b)/(a + b)**
- Expanding brackets: 5(2 + x) = 10 + 5x; (x + 3)(x + 4) = x² + 7x + 12
- Factorising: 2x − 4 = 2(x − 2); xy + 2x = x(y + 2)

## Questions
> [!question]- Make x the subject of ax + b(x − a) = ay.
