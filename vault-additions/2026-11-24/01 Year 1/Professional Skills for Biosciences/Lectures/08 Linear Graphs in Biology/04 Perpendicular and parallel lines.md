# Perpendicular and parallel lines

> [!path] [[Professional Skills for Biosciences]] › [[Linear Graphs in Biology (Maths 6)]]

*Source: Mathematical Concepts: Linear Graphs in Biology, Dr Beth Williams (PowerPoint), slides 22-25*

- **Perpendicular** (90°): m₁ × m₂ = **−1** → m₂ = −1/m₁ ("flip and change sign")
- **Parallel**: same gradient, m₁ = m₂; only the intercept differs

## Examples using 3x − 4y − 2 = 0 → y = ¾x − ½ (m = ¾)
- Perpendicular through the origin: m = −4/3, c = 0 → **y = −4x/3** (3y + 4x = 0)
- Parallel through (1, 2): ¾ = (y − 2)/(x − 1) → 4y − 8 = 3x − 3 → **y = ¾x + 5/4**

## Questions
> [!question]- A line has gradient ¾. Gradient of a perpendicular line?
> −4/3.

> [!question]- Line parallel to y = ¾x − ½ through (1, 2)?
> y = ¾x + 5/4.
