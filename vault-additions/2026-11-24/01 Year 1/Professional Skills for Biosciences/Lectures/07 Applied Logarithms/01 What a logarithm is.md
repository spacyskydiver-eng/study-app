# What a logarithm is

> [!path] [[Professional Skills for Biosciences]] › [[Applied Logarithms (Maths 5)]]

*Source: Mathematical Concepts: Applied Logarithms, Dr Beth Williams (PowerPoint), slides 2-9*

- A **logarithm** is the inverse of a power: the number the base must be raised to, to give the number
- **log_a x = y ⇔ aʸ = x** ("the logarithm of x with base a is y")
- 1000 = 10³ → log₁₀ 1000 = **3**; 10 = e²·³ → ln 10 = **2.3**
- To remove a log, raise the base to both sides: log x = 4 → **x = 10⁴**; log₂ 128 = y → 128 = 2ʸ (y = 7); ln z = 2 → **z = e²**

## Why logs in biology
- Many biological relationships are **non-linear**; logging turns them into **straight lines**
- From a straight line, the **x- and y-intercepts** can be read accurately, and they are biologically meaningful
- Data spanning 1 to 10⁶ is unreadable on a normal axis but a straight line on a log axis
