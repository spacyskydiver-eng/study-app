# The CFU calculation

> [!path] [[Professional Skills for Biosciences]] › [[CFU and Dilutions (Maths 3)]]

*Source: Mathematical Concepts: CFU and Dilutions (PowerPoint, Colony Forming Units), slides 8-13*

> [!key] Viable count
> **CFU mL⁻¹ = (number of colonies × dilution factor) / volume plated (mL)**

- The dilution factor is the **positive** power: a 10⁻⁶ dilution → × 10⁶
- Volume plated must be in **mL**: 100 µL = 0.1 mL; 50 µL = 0.05 mL; 10 µL = 0.01 mL

## Lake water
- 100 µL of the 10⁻⁶ dilution gave **50 colonies**
- (50 × 10⁶) / 0.1 mL = **5 × 10⁸ CFU mL⁻¹**

## Yoghurt drink
- Dilutions 1 to 10⁻⁷; 10 µL plated; 10⁻⁶ plate had **65 colonies**
- (65 × 10⁶) / 0.01 mL = **6.5 × 10⁹ CFU mL⁻¹**

## CFU per gram (workbook)
- 0.1 g soil in 1.0 mL; 50 µL plated; 10⁻⁵ plate had 57 colonies (in the 30 to 300 range)
- 57 × 10⁵ / 0.05 = 1.14 × 10⁸ CFU mL⁻¹ of suspension → ÷ 0.1 g = **1.1 × 10⁹ CFU g⁻¹**

## Questions
> [!question]- 100 µL of a 10⁻⁶ dilution gives 50 colonies. CFU mL⁻¹ of the original?
> (50 × 10⁶)/0.1 = 5 × 10⁸ CFU mL⁻¹.

> [!question]- 10 µL of a 10⁻⁶ dilution gives 65 colonies. CFU mL⁻¹?
> (65 × 10⁶)/0.01 = 6.5 × 10⁹ CFU mL⁻¹.
