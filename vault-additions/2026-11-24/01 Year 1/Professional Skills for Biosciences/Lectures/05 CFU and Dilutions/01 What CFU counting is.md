# What CFU counting is

> [!path] [[Professional Skills for Biosciences]] › [[CFU and Dilutions (Maths 3)]]

*Source: Mathematical Concepts: CFU and Dilutions (PowerPoint, Colony Forming Units), slides 2-7*

- **CFU mL⁻¹** is a way of counting bacteria in a sample, e.g. coliform bacteria in drinking water
1. The sample is **serially diluted** (usually 10-fold steps)
2. A **known volume** of each dilution is plated on suitable medium
3. After incubation the **colonies are counted**
- Each colony grows from **one bacterial cell**, so colonies × dilution factor ÷ volume plated gives the original concentration
- To work back you need the **dilution factor** and the **volume plated**

## Limitations
- Relies on culturing: only around **1 %** of bacteria can be cultured
- Only counts **live** (viable) bacteria

## Questions
> [!question]- Why is the count called colony-forming units rather than cells?
> Each colony grows from one viable cell (or clump), and only bacteria that can grow on the plate are counted.
