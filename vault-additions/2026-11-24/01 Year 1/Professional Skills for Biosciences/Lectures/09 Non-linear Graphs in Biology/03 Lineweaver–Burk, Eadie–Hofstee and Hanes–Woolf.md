# Lineweaver–Burk, Eadie–Hofstee and Hanes–Woolf

> [!path] [[Professional Skills for Biosciences]] › [[Non-linear Graphs in Biology (Maths 7)]]

*Source: Mathematical Concepts: Non-linear Graphs in Biology (PowerPoint), slides 11-13*

## Lineweaver–Burk (most common)
1. Invert: 1/V = (Km + [S]) / (Vmax[S])
2. Split: **1/V = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax**

| Plot | y | x | Gradient | y-intercept | x-intercept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lineweaver–Burk** | 1/V | 1/[S] | Km/Vmax | 1/Vmax | −1/Km |
| **Eadie–Hofstee** | V | V/[S] | −Km | Vmax | Vmax/Km |
| **Hanes–Woolf** | [S]/V | [S] | 1/Vmax | Km/Vmax | −Km |

- x-intercept: set y = 0 and rearrange (a theoretical value)
- Eadie–Hofstee is more complicated but avoids the measurement errors that inverting magnifies; Hanes–Woolf avoids inverting everything

## Monod equation (microbial growth)
- **µ = µmax [S] / (Ks + [S])**: µ specific growth rate, µmax maximum, [S] limiting nutrient, Ks = [S] at µmax/2
- Inverted: **1/µ = (Ks/µmax)(1/[S]) + 1/µmax** → gradient Ks/µmax, y-intercept 1/µmax

## Questions
> [!question]- On a Lineweaver–Burk plot, what are the y- and x-intercepts?
> y-intercept 1/Vmax; x-intercept −1/Km.

> [!question]- What is plotted on a Hanes–Woolf plot?
> [S]/V against [S].
