# Law of independent assortment

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Mendelian Inheritance - Independent Assortment (Lecture 18)]]

*Source: Lecture 18: Mendelian inheritance - independent assortment (PowerPoint), slides 10-12*

> [!key] Mendel's second law
> **Each pair of alleles assorts independently of each other pair of alleles during gamete formation.**

## Where it happens: meiosis I
- Homologous pairs line up at the metaphase plate **randomly**
- For two pairs there are **two equally likely arrangements**:
  - Le with I on one side, le with i on the other → gametes **Le I** and **le i**
  - Le with i on one side, le with I on the other → gametes **Le i** and **le I**
- Over many meioses this gives all **four gamete types in equal proportions**

## Gamete combinations
- With **n** pairs of chromosomes there are **2ⁿ** combinations

| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 23 (human) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2ⁿ | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | **8,388,608** |

- Applies to genes on **different chromosomes** (or very far apart on one); linked genes break the rule (see [[Linkage Analysis practical]])

## Questions
> [!question]- State the law of independent assortment.
> Each pair of alleles assorts independently of each other pair of alleles during gamete formation.

> [!question]- At which stage of meiosis does independent assortment happen?
> Metaphase/anaphase I, when homologous pairs line up randomly and separate.

> [!question]- How many chromosome combinations can a human gamete have from independent assortment alone?
> 2²³ = 8,388,608.
