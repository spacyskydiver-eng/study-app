# Product law: phenotype probabilities

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Mendelian Inheritance - Independent Assortment (Lecture 18)]]

*Source: Lecture 18: Mendelian inheritance - independent assortment (PowerPoint), slides 20-27*

> [!key] Product law
> When two independent events occur together, the combined probability is the **product of their individual probabilities**.

- Two coins: P(heads and heads) = ½ × ½ = ¼
- In a dihybrid cross, if the genes assort independently, each gene behaves as a **separate monohybrid cross** (3 : 1 each)

## Example 1: tall **and** yellow
- P(tall) = ¾, P(yellow) = ¾
- **¾ × ¾ = 9/16** (0.5625)

## Example 2: tall **and** green
- P(tall) = ¾, P(green) = ¼
- **¾ × ¼ = 3/16** (0.1875)

## Expected numbers
- Multiply the probability by the total offspring
- Tall round × dwarf wrinkled, 1600 F₂: tall wrinkled = 3/16 × 1600 = **300**
- Red eyes ¾, long wings ¾ in fruit flies: red **and** long = **9/16**

## Questions
> [!question]- Using the product law, what is the probability an F₂ plant is dwarf and yellow?
> P(dwarf) × P(yellow) = ¼ × ¾ = 3/16.

> [!question]- From 1600 F₂ of tall round × dwarf wrinkled, how many tall wrinkled are expected?
> 3/16 × 1600 = 300.

> [!question]- In fruit flies P(red eyes) = ¾ and P(long wings) = ¾. What is P(red and long)?
> 9/16.
