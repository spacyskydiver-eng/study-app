# Mitosis and the cell cycle

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Cell Division (Lecture 16)]]

*Source: Lecture 16: Chromosomes and cell division (PowerPoint), slides 3-5, 7-8*

## Mitosis in one line
- **[[Mitosis]]**: **1 diploid cell → 2 diploid daughter cells**, genetically identical to the parent
- Happens in **[[Somatic cell|somatic cells]]**: part of normal growth and development

## The cell cycle
- **Interphase** = **G1 → S → G2**, then **M** (mitosis)
  - **G1**: growth
  - **S phase (synthesis)**: every chromosome is **replicated**, so each becomes two **[[Sister chromatids|sister chromatids]]** joined at the **[[Centromere|centromere]]**
  - **G2**: preparation for division
- **G0**: cells that have stopped dividing (non-dividing cells) leave G1 into G0
- Typical human cell in culture: **G1 5 h, S 7 h, G2 3 h, M 1 h**. Within M: prophase ≈ 36 min, metaphase 3, anaphase 3, telophase 18 min
- Mitosis is the **shortest** part of the cycle

> [!tip] Chromatids vs chromosomes
> After S phase a chromosome looks like an **X**: two **sister chromatids** (identical copies). The matching chromosome from the other parent is its **[[Homologous chromosomes|homologue]]**; chromatids on different homologues are **non-sister chromatids**.

## Questions
> [!question]- What does mitosis produce?
> Two diploid daughter cells genetically identical to the parent cell.

> [!question]- In which phase of the cell cycle is DNA replicated?
> S phase (synthesis), part of interphase.

> [!question]- List the phases of the cell cycle in order.
> G1 → S → G2 → M (mitosis); cells that stop dividing enter G0 from G1.

> [!question]- What are sister chromatids?
> The two identical copies of a chromosome made in S phase, joined at the centromere.
