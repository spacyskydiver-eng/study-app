# Genetic diversity from meiosis

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Cell Division (Lecture 16)]]

*Source: Lecture 16: Chromosomes and cell division (PowerPoint), slides 21-25*

## 1. Independent assortment
- In metaphase I each homologous pair lines up **independently** of the others
- With **n = 2** (pairs 1 and 2, alleles 1a/1b and 2a/2b) there are **two** ways to line up, giving gametes **1a 2a, 1b 2b** or **1a 2b, 1b 2a**: **4** combinations
- Number of combinations = **2ⁿ**: humans (n = 23) → 2²³ ≈ **8.4 million** before crossing over

## 2. Recombination (crossing over)
- In **prophase I**, **non-sister chromatids** of a homologous pair exchange segments at a **chiasma** (crossover point)
- A chromosome carrying **A B** paired with **a b** can give **A b** and **a B** chromatids as well as the parental **A B** and **a b**
- So genes on the **same chromosome** can be split up: the basis of **linkage mapping** (see [[Linkage Analysis practical]])

## Questions
> [!question]- Name the two sources of genetic diversity in meiosis.
> Independent assortment of homologous pairs, and recombination (crossing over) between non-sister chromatids.

> [!question]- How many different chromosome combinations can a human gamete have from independent assortment alone?
> 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million.

> [!question]- When and between what does crossing over happen?
> Prophase I, between non-sister chromatids of a homologous pair, at chiasmata.

> [!question]- A cell is A B / a b on one pair. Which gametes show crossing over happened between A and B?
> A b and a B (recombinant).
