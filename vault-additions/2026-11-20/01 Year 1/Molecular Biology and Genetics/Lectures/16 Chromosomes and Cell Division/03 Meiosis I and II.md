# Meiosis I and II

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Chromosomes and Cell Division (Lecture 16)]]

*Source: Lecture 16: Chromosomes and cell division (PowerPoint), slides 14-19*

## Meiosis in one line
- **[[Meiosis]]**: **1 diploid cell (2n) → 4 haploid gametes (1n)**, each with **half** the chromosome number
- Happens in **reproductive cells**; leads to **[[Gamete|gametes]]**
- **n** = the **haploid number** of chromosomes (human n = 23; mouse 20; fruit fly 4)

## The steps
1. **S phase**: DNA is replicated; each chromosome becomes two sister chromatids. Homologous pairs come together as a **[[Bivalent|bivalent]]** (tetrad)
2. **Meiosis I** (first division, **reductional**): **homologous pairs separate** into two daughter cells
   - Prophase I: homologues pair (**synapsis**) and can **cross over** at **chiasmata**
   - Metaphase I: **pairs** line up on the plate
   - Anaphase I: **homologues** (not chromatids) go to opposite poles
3. **Meiosis II** (second division, **equational**): **centromeres divide** and **sister chromatids** separate, like mitosis
4. Result: **4 haploid gametes**

## Human gametogenesis
- **Spermatogenesis**: primary spermatocyte → 2 secondary spermatocytes → **4 spermatids** → 4 sperm
- **Oogenesis**: primary oocyte → secondary oocyte + **first polar body** → ootid + **second polar body** → **1 ovum** (the polar bodies don't become eggs)

## Questions
> [!question]- How many cells does meiosis produce, and what ploidy are they?
> Four haploid (1n) gametes.

> [!question]- Why is meiosis I called the reductional division?
> Homologous chromosomes separate, halving the chromosome number.

> [!question]- What separates in meiosis II?
> Sister chromatids, after the centromeres divide.

> [!question]- How many functional eggs come from one primary oocyte?
> One ovum; the other products are polar bodies.
