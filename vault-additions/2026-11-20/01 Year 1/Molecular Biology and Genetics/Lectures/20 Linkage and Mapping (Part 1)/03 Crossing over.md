# Crossing over

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Linkage and Mapping Part 1 (Lecture 20)]]

*Source: Lecture 20: Linkage and mapping (Part 1) (PowerPoint), slides 13, 19-20*

- Happens in **prophase I** of meiosis, when homologues pair to form a **tetrad** (4 chromatids)
- Non-sister chromatids break and rejoin at a **chiasma** (plural chiasmata), the visible crossover point
- Result = **recombination**: pairing, breaking and joining of chromosomes

## Following it through (y⁺ w⁺ / y w)
1. **Chromosome replication**: each homologue becomes 2 sister chromatids
2. **Recombination**: one y⁺ w⁺ chromatid swaps its w end with a y w chromatid
3. **First meiotic division**: homologues separate
4. **Second meiotic division**: sister chromatids separate → 4 gametes
   - 2 **non-crossover** (parental): y⁺ w⁺ and y w
   - 2 **crossover (recombinant)**: y⁺ w and y w⁺
- One crossover affects only 2 of the 4 chromatids, so at most half the gametes from that meiosis are recombinant

## Questions
> [!question]- When and where does crossing over happen?
> Prophase I of meiosis, between non-sister chromatids of paired homologues, at chiasmata.

> [!question]- From one meiosis with a single crossover between two genes, how many of the four gametes are recombinant?
> Two (the two chromatids involved); the other two are parental.
