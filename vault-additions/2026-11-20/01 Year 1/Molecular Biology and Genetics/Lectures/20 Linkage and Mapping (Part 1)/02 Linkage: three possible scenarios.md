# Linkage: three possible scenarios

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Linkage and Mapping Part 1 (Lecture 20)]]

*Source: Lecture 20: Linkage and mapping (Part 1) (PowerPoint), slides 9-12*

Two genes **A** and **B**, parent heterozygous at both loci (AaBb):

| Scenario | Where the genes are | Gametes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unlinked** | different chromosomes, or far apart on one | AB : Ab : aB : ab = **1 : 1 : 1 : 1** (what Mendel saw) |
| **Complete linkage** | same chromosome, no crossing over | **only parental** gametes (AB and ab); gives a "linkage ratio" in the F₂ |
| **Incomplete linkage** | same chromosome, crossing over happens | parental gametes (AB, ab) **predominate** = **noncrossover gametes**; non-parental (Ab, aB) also made = **crossover gametes** |

- **Linkage** modifies Mendelian ratios: segregation still applies but **assortment is not independent**

## Questions
> [!question]- What gametes does an AB/ab parent make if A and B are completely linked?
> Only the parental gametes AB and ab.

> [!question]- What are crossover gametes?
> Gametes with a non-parental combination of alleles (e.g. Ab and aB from an AB/ab parent), made by crossing over.

> [!question]- What gamete ratio do unlinked genes give from an AaBb parent?
> AB : Ab : aB : ab = 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.
