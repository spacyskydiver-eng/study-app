# Interference and the coefficient of coincidence

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Linkage and Mapping Part 2 (Lecture 21)]]

*Source: Lecture 21: Linkage and mapping (Part 2) (PowerPoint), slides 25-28, 31-34*

- **Interference**: the frequency of double crossovers is **greater or less than expected**
  - **Negative interference**: more DCOs than expected
  - **Positive interference**: fewer DCOs than expected

## Expected DCO
- Multiply the single-interval probabilities. Maize: v –22.3– pr –43.4– bm
- DCO_exp = 0.223 × 0.434 = **0.097 = 9.7 %**

## Coefficient of coincidence
> C = **observed DCO ÷ expected DCO**
- Observed 7.8 % → C = 0.078 ÷ 0.097 = **0.804**

## Interference
> I = **1 − C**
- Maize: I = 1 − 0.804 = **0.196** → 19.6 % fewer DCOs than expected

| I | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| positive | fewer DCOs than expected (positive interference) |
| negative | more DCOs than expected |
| 0 | observed = expected (no interference) |

- In eukaryotes **positive interference** is usual: one crossover seems to **inhibit** a second crossover nearby

## Questions
> [!question]- Two intervals are 10 cM and 20 cM. What DCO frequency is expected?
> 0.1 × 0.2 = 0.02 = 2 %.

> [!question]- Expected DCO 2 %, observed 1 %. Give C and I.
> C = 0.5; I = 0.5 (positive interference).

> [!question]- What does positive interference mean?
> Fewer double crossovers than expected; one crossover inhibits another nearby.
