# Mendel's experimental approach

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Mendelian Inheritance - Allelic Segregation (Lecture 17)]]

*Source: Lecture 17: Mendelian inheritance - allelic segregation (PowerPoint), slides 4-5*

- **Gregor Mendel** (1822–1884): research 1856–1868, results published **1865**
- Used the **garden pea** (*Pisum sativum*) because it is:
  - easy to grow, matures in **one season**
  - naturally **self-fertile** (hermaphrodite) but easy to **cross by hand**
  - available in **true-breeding** strains (always breed true for a trait)
- Studied **seven characters**, each with **two contrasting phenotypes**:

| Character | Phenotypes |
| --- | --- |
| Seed shape | round / wrinkled |
| Seed colour | yellow / green |
| Pod shape | full / constricted |
| Pod colour | green / yellow |
| Flower colour | violet / white |
| Flower position | axial / terminal |
| Stem length | tall / dwarf |

- Kept it simple: **one or a few characters** at a time, and recorded **numbers** (quantitative data)

## Questions
> [!question]- Why was the garden pea a good organism for Mendel?
> Easy to grow, one season to maturity, self-fertile but easy to cross by hand, and true-breeding strains with clear contrasting traits.

> [!question]- What does true-breeding mean?
> A strain that always produces offspring with the same trait when self-fertilised (it is homozygous).
