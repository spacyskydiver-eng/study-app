# Test crosses and pedigrees

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Mendelian Inheritance - Allelic Segregation (Lecture 17)]]

*Source: Lecture 17: Mendelian inheritance - allelic segregation (PowerPoint), slides 24-27*

## What a 3 : 1 ratio tells you
- A **3 : 1** F₂ ratio means the trait is controlled by **one gene with two alleles**, one **dominant**
- Example: disease **resistance** (R) × **susceptible** (r) in *Arabidopsis* gives F₂ **3 resistant : 1 susceptible** (the *RPW8* mildew-resistance gene; Xiao *et al.*, *Science* 2001)

## Test cross
- Tells **homozygotes from heterozygotes** among individuals with the **dominant** phenotype
- Cross the dominant individual with a **homozygous recessive** (rr)
  - **RR × rr** → all **Rr**: **all resistant**
  - **Rr × rr** → **½ Rr : ½ rr**: **1 resistant : 1 susceptible**

## Human pedigrees
- Humans can't be crossed, so inheritance is followed in **family trees**: squares = males, circles = females, filled = affected
- For a **recessive** trait, two unaffected parents (**Aa × Aa**) can have an affected child (**aa**)
- The **propositus** is the affected person who brought the family to attention

## Questions
> [!question]- What is a test cross for?
> To find whether an individual with the dominant phenotype is homozygous (AA) or heterozygous (Aa).

> [!question]- A resistant plant crossed to rr gives half resistant and half susceptible offspring. What is its genotype?
> Rr (heterozygous).

> [!question]- What does an F₂ ratio of 3 : 1 suggest?
> One gene with two alleles, one completely dominant.

> [!question]- Two unaffected parents have an affected child. What does that suggest about the trait?
> It is recessive and both parents are heterozygous carriers (Aa × Aa).
