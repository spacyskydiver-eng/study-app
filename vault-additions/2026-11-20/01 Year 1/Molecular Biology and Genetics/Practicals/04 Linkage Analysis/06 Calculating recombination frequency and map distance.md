# Calculating recombination frequency and map distance

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Linkage Analysis practical]]

*Source: Practical Manual 2 4018A (Genetics and transformation), page 8*

## The calculation
1. Decide whether your mouse is **i, ii, iii or iv** from its gel
2. Record it on the **shared spreadsheet**
3. Once everyone's data is in:

> [!key] Recombination frequency
> **RF = number of recombinant progeny ÷ total number of progeny**
>
> Map distance (cM) = RF × 100, because **1% recombination = 1 centiMorgan**

- Recombinant progeny are genotypes **iii and iv**; non-recombinant are **i and ii**
- For close loci the observed RF is a good estimate of the real distance

> [!example] Worked example (made-up class data)
> 48 progeny: 20 type i, 19 type ii, 5 type iii, 4 type iv.
> 1. Recombinants = 5 + 4 = **9**
> 2. RF = 9 / 48 = **0.1875**
> 3. Distance = 0.1875 × 100 = **18.75 cM**, about **19 cM** between M1 and M2

## Checking your answer
- RF can't be more than **0.5** (50%). Unlinked genes give **about 50% recombinant** progeny, so an RF near 0.5 means M1 and M2 behave as if unlinked
- Non-recombinants should **outnumber** recombinants if the markers are linked
- Small classes give rough estimates: one more recombinant in 48 changes the RF by about 2%

## Questions
> [!question]- Write the formula for recombination frequency.
> RF = number of recombinant progeny ÷ total number of progeny.

> [!question]- How is RF converted to a map distance?
> Multiply by 100: 1% recombination = 1 centiMorgan.

> [!question]- 60 progeny: 26 type i, 25 type ii, 5 type iii and 4 type iv. What is the M1–M2 distance?
> Recombinants = 5 + 4 = 9. RF = 9 / 60 = 0.15, so 15 cM.

> [!question]- The class finds 50% recombinant progeny. What does that suggest?
> M1 and M2 assort independently: they are unlinked, or so far apart on one chromosome that crossovers almost always separate them.

> [!question]- Why does observed RF underestimate the distance for loci far apart?
> Two crossovers between the loci put the original alleles back together, so those progeny look non-recombinant and are missed.
