# Aim and background

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Linkage Analysis practical]]

*Source: Practical Manual 2 4018A (Genetics and transformation), page 5*

## The idea
- Two loci are **[[Genetic linkage|linked]]** if they sit close together on the **same chromosome**, so they tend to be inherited together
- They are not always inherited together: **[[Crossing over|crossing over]]** in [[Meiosis|meiosis]] can swap alleles between homologous chromosomes
- The further apart two loci are, the more likely a crossover falls between them
- So the **[[Recombination frequency|recombination frequency]] (RF)** between two loci tells you their **genetic distance**

## What you do
- Two **[[Molecular marker|molecular markers]]** in mouse, **M1** and **M2**, each with two alleles (a and b)
- You get DNA from **one progeny mouse** of a cross, run a **[[Polymerase chain reaction|PCR]]** for M1 and one for M2, and read its genotype from a **[[Gel electrophoresis|gel]]**
- Everyone in the group has a different progeny mouse. Pooling everyone's genotypes gives the RF, and from that the distance between M1 and M2 in **[[Centimorgan|centiMorgans]]**

## Questions
> [!question]- When are two genetic loci said to be linked?
> When they are close together on the same chromosome, so they tend to be inherited together.

> [!question]- What does the recombination frequency between two loci tell you?
> Their genetic distance: the further apart they are, the more often a crossover separates them, so the higher the RF.

> [!question]- In this practical, what do you analyse and how is the class data used?
> One progeny mouse's DNA, by M1 and M2 PCR. Everyone's genotypes are pooled to calculate the recombination frequency and the M1–M2 distance.
