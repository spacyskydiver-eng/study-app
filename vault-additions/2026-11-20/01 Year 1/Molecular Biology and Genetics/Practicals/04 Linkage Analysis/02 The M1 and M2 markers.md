# The M1 and M2 markers

> [!path] [[Molecular Biology and Genetics]] › [[Linkage Analysis practical]]

*Source: Practical Manual 2 4018A (Genetics and transformation), page 5, Table 1 and Figure 1*

## Length polymorphisms
- Each marker has two alleles whose PCR products differ in **length** (a **[[Length polymorphism|length polymorphism]]**), so they separate on a gel

| Locus | Allele | Primers | PCR product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | M1a | M1F + M1R | **521 bp** |
| M1 | M1b | M1F + M1R | **761 bp** |
| M2 | M2a | M2F + M2R | **1020 bp** |
| M2 | M2b | M2F + M2R | **1500 bp** |

- The **same primer pair** amplifies both alleles of a marker, so one M1 PCR shows whether a mouse has M1a, M1b or both
- A **[[Homozygous|homozygote]]** (e.g. M1a/M1a) gives **one band**; a **[[Heterozygous|heterozygote]]** (M1a/M1b) gives **two bands**

> [!tip] Remember the sizes
> In both markers the **a** allele is the **shorter** product: M1a 521 < M1b 761, and M2a 1020 < M2b 1500.

## Questions
> [!question]- What size PCR products do M1a, M1b, M2a and M2b give?
> M1a 521 bp, M1b 761 bp, M2a 1020 bp, M2b 1500 bp.

> [!question]- Why can one primer pair tell apart both alleles of M1?
> The primers bind either side of a length polymorphism, so the M1a and M1b products are different lengths (521 and 761 bp) and separate on a gel.

> [!question]- How many bands does a heterozygote give in one marker's PCR, and why?
> Two: one product from each allele, because the two alleles give different lengths.
