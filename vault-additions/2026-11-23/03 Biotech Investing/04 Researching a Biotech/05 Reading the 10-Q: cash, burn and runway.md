# Reading the 10-Q: cash, burn and runway

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

- A [[Form 10-Q]] is filed every quarter (a [[Form 10-K]] once a year, with the fourth quarter). For a company with no sales, three numbers matter most: cash, [[Cash burn]] and [[Cash runway]]

## Where the numbers are
| Number | Where in the 10-Q |
| --- | --- |
| Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities (investments) | Balance sheet, first lines under "Current assets" (add any long-term investments too) |
| Net cash used in operating activities | Cash-flow statement, first subtotal. Use this for burn, not net loss (net loss includes non-cash items such as share-based pay) |
| Net loss | Statement of operations, bottom line |
| The company's own runway statement | "Liquidity and capital resources" in the MD&A, or Note 1 |
| [[Going concern]] warning | Note 1. If it is there, the company doubts it can last 12 months without new money |

## Worked example: Hemab Therapeutics (COAG), 10-Q to 30 Sep 2026
1. Cash: $239.1m cash and equivalents + $191.1m marketable securities = **$430.2m**
2. Burn: net cash used in operating activities over 9 months = $72.5m, so per quarter = 72.5 ÷ 3 = **$24.2m**
3. Runway: 430.2 ÷ 24.2 = **17.8 quarters ≈ 4.4 years**
4. Adjust: Hemab started a Phase 3 trial in October 2026. Phase 3 costs much more than Phase 2, so the real runway is shorter. If burn doubles to ~$48m a quarter: 430.2 ÷ 48 ≈ 9 quarters ≈ 2.2 years
5. Compare with the next milestones: the Phase 3 (SOLARA) is not due to finish until about Nov 2029, later than the adjusted runway. So Hemab will very likely raise more money before then

## Method for any company
1. Cash = cash and equivalents + all marketable securities / investments
2. Quarterly burn = 9-month operating cash used ÷ 3 (or 6-month ÷ 2, or the full year ÷ 4)
3. Runway in quarters = cash ÷ quarterly burn
4. Adjust burn up if a big trial is starting
5. Is the runway past the next big readout? If not, expect [[Share dilution]] before the result

## What dilution means for you
- Raising money usually means selling new shares, often at a discount. Each existing share then owns a smaller slice of the company
- Not always bad: money raised after good data, at a higher price, is healthy. Money raised in a hurry after bad data is the painful kind

## Questions
> [!question]- Why use operating cash flow rather than net loss for burn?
> Net loss includes non-cash costs such as share-based pay; operating cash flow is the money actually leaving the bank.

> [!question]- A company has $300m and used $90m in operating activities over 9 months. What is its runway?
> Quarterly burn = 90 ÷ 3 = $30m. Runway = 300 ÷ 30 = 10 quarters, 2.5 years.

> [!question]- What does a going concern warning in Note 1 mean?
> Management doubts the company can keep going for the next 12 months without new money; a share sale, deal or cuts are likely soon.
