# Scorecard, thesis and sell rules

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

## Scorecard (score each 0, 1 or 2)
| Area | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Science | weak or unproven target | plausible, early data | strong genetics or proven class, good controlled data |
| Stage | preclinical or Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Phase 3 or filed |
| Competition | crowded, no clear edge | some edge | first or clearly best |
| Runway | under 18 months | 18 to 36 months | over 3 years, past the next readout |
| Valuation | success already priced in | fair | well below the risk-adjusted value |
| Management and backers | unknown, no specialist funds | some track record | proven team, specialist funds own shares |

- 9 or more: worth deeper work. Under 6: pass. In between: watchlist
- A score is a way to compare companies side by side, not a verdict

## Red flags
- [[Going concern]] warning
- Runway ends before the next big readout
- Repeated changes of trial endpoint or timeline
- Only one drug and a crowded market
- Management that talks about the share price more than the data

## Writing the [[Investment thesis]]
- Two or three sentences: why this company should be worth much more in 3 to 5 years, and what has to happen
- Example layout: "[Company] should be worth more because [drug] can [advantage] in [disease]. It needs [event] by [date]. The thesis breaks if [result] or [cash problem]."

## Sizing and selling
- [[Position sizing]]: a small slice of the satellite pot per company (e.g. 2 to 5% of the total portfolio), spread over 5 to 10 companies, because most small biotechs fail
- Sell when the **reason** breaks (trial fails, cash runs out, a better drug arrives), not when the price wobbles
- Review each holding after every 10-Q and every trial result: is the thesis still true?

## Questions
> [!question]- Name three red flags in a small biotech.
> Any three of: going concern warning; runway shorter than the time to the next readout; repeated endpoint or timeline changes; one drug in a crowded market; management focused on the share price.

> [!question]- When should a long-term position be sold?
> When the reason for owning it breaks (trial fails, cash runs out, a better drug arrives), not because the price falls.
