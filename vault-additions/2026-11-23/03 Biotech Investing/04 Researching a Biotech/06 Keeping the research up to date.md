# Keeping the research up to date

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

## What the site does by itself
- New filings appear on the company's **Filings** line and as **F** markers on the chart and in Updates, usually within half a day of being filed
- After each new 10-Q or 10-K, cash, burn, runway, revenue, net loss, shares, market cap and enterprise value are recalculated from the filing's tagged data
- Trial registry changes, new papers and news are checked several times a day, as before

## What to do when a new 10-Q appears (about 20 minutes)
1. Open it from the Filings line
2. Check the automatic figures against the balance sheet and cash flow statement once, so you trust them
3. Cash: did it fall by about the expected burn? If it rose, find the financing line (new shares sold?)
4. Shares outstanding: up more than a few percent means [[Share dilution]]
5. R&D: a jump usually means a trial started. Check the MD&A for which
6. MD&A: any change to timelines or the runway statement ("into 2028" becoming "into 2027" is a warning)
7. Risk factors: anything new
8. Rewrite the [[Investment thesis]] line if anything changed, and re-score the scorecard

## When an 8-K appears
- Open it and Exhibit 99.1 (the press release). For trial results, read the numbers, not the headline: [[08 Reading a trial press release|Reading a trial press release]]

## When the figures look wrong
- Some companies tag their numbers differently, so a figure can be missing or include something odd (for example restricted cash). The filing itself is always the source of truth
- Runway assumes spending stays the same. Before a Phase 3 starts or a drug launches, spending usually rises, so the real runway is shorter

## Questions
> [!question]- After a new 10-Q, shares outstanding rose 20%. What does it mean?
> The company sold new shares (or converted other securities), diluting existing shareholders; check the financing section and subsequent events for why and at what price.

> [!question]- The runway statement changed from "into 2028" to "into 2027". Why does it matter?
> Spending has risen or plans changed, so the company will need money sooner, raising the chance of a share sale before its next result.
