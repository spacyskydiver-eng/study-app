# Reading the prospectus

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

- The [[Prospectus]] is written when a company first sells shares ([[IPO]]). It is the most complete description the company will ever give: 200 to 300 pages, but only five parts need reading
- On EDGAR it is the **S-1** (draft, can be amended as S-1/A) or the **424B4** (final, with the price)

## The five parts, in this order
1. **Prospectus summary** (first 10 to 15 pages): the company in brief, the pipeline chart, the strategy. Note the lead drug, the disease and the stage
2. **Business → our programs**: the science, animal and early human data, the trial plan. Note the mechanism and the actual numbers from trials (how many patients, what changed, versus placebo or not)
3. **Business → competition**: who else is in the disease. A long list of big companies with approved drugs means the new drug has to be clearly better
4. **Use of proceeds**: what the IPO money pays for and how far it gets them. "Sufficient to fund operations into the second half of 2029" is a runway statement
5. **Risk factors**: long and mostly standard. Read the first few pages and anything specific to this company (one drug only, a partner who controls sales, manufacturing problems)

## Also worth a glance
- **Principal stockholders**: who owns it. Specialist biotech funds owning a lot is a mild good sign: they have done deep research
- **[[Lock-up period]]**: insiders usually cannot sell for 180 days after the IPO. When it ends, extra shares can hit the market and the price often dips
- **Licence agreements**: whether the drug was licensed from another company, and what royalties or milestones are owed on sales

## Example: Lycia Therapeutics (LYCA)
- One sentence: a Phase 1 drug, LCA-0061, that degrades IgE antibodies, aimed at food allergy
- Runway: the IPO money plus existing cash is "sufficient... into the second half of 2029"
- Next: more Phase 1 data in 2027. Phase 1 drugs reach approval only about 8% of the time, so this is a very early, high-risk bet

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the difference between an S-1 and a 424B4?
> The S-1 is the registration statement (draft, can be amended); the 424B4 is the final prospectus with the price and number of shares sold.

> [!question]- Which five parts of a prospectus are worth reading?
> The summary, the programs (science and trial data), competition, use of proceeds, and risk factors.

> [!question]- Why can the share price dip about 180 days after an IPO?
> The lock-up ends, so insiders and early investors are allowed to sell their shares.
