# The SEC documents explained

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

- Every company listed on a US exchange must publish its reports with the **SEC** (Securities and Exchange Commission, the US market regulator). They are free on [[EDGAR]], the SEC's filing database
- The company writes them, but lying in them is illegal and auditors check the yearly numbers, so they are the most reliable source there is: more reliable than news, press releases or social media

## The filings, and what each is for
| Form | When | What it is | What you use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S-1** / **424B4** ([[Prospectus]]) | Once, when the company lists ([[IPO]]) or sells new shares | The full description: science, pipeline, competitors, finances, risks, owners | Understanding the company from scratch. Read first |
| **[[Form 10-Q]]** | Every quarter, about 6 weeks after the quarter ends (not for Q4) | Quarterly financial statements (not audited) and management's commentary | Cash, spending, runway, what changed since last quarter |
| **[[Form 10-K]]** | Once a year, about 2 months after the year ends | The annual report: audited statements, the whole business and pipeline, all risk factors | The yearly full check. Replaces the prospectus as the best overview once the company is older |
| **[[Form 8-K]]** | Within 4 business days of a major event | Short notice of an event: results (often with the press release as Exhibit 99.1), trial data, deals, new shares sold, leadership changes | Finding out what happened, with the exact wording. Trial results usually appear here first |
| **DEF 14A** (proxy) | Before the yearly shareholder meeting | Executive pay, board members, votes | Whether management is paid for real progress |
| **[[Form 4]]** | Within 2 business days of an insider trade | Directors and executives buying or selling their own company's shares | Insider buying with their own money is a mildly positive sign; selling is common and often planned in advance |
| **13D / 13G** | When someone passes 5% ownership | A big investor's stake | Who the major owners are (specialist biotech funds, other drug companies) |
| **20-F / 6-K** | Non-US companies | The foreign versions of the 10-K and of the 10-Q/8-K | Same use, for companies such as BioNTech |

## Finding them, step by step
On the site:
1. Open [Markets](#/markets) and tap the company's card
2. Under the chart name, the **Filings** line links the latest quarterly report, annual report, prospectus and 8-K, with their dates. These update automatically (below)
3. **All filings** opens the full list on EDGAR

On EDGAR directly:
1. Go to sec.gov/edgar/search and type the company name or ticker (e.g. COAG)
2. On the company's page, use **Filing type** to filter (type 10-Q)
3. Click a filing, then the main document (the first .htm in the list, usually named like `coag-20260930.htm`)
4. Use the browser's Find (Ctrl+F / Cmd+F) to jump to sections: "Balance sheets", "cash used in operating", "sufficient to fund", "going concern", "Risk factors"

## What updates by itself on the site
- Twice a day the site checks EDGAR for each company's newest filings. A new 10-Q, 10-K, 8-K or prospectus appears on the **Filings** line and as an **F** (Filing) marker on the chart and in Updates
- After each new quarterly report, the site reads the figures the company tags in its filing (XBRL data) and recalculates: cash and investments, quarterly burn, runway, last quarter's revenue and net loss, shares outstanding, market cap and enterprise value. These show under the company name, with the report date
- Things that stay manual: the stage, the next milestone and the notes, which need reading and judgement

## Questions
> [!question]- Which filing would first show a Phase 3 result?
> An 8-K, filed within four business days, usually with the press release attached as Exhibit 99.1.

> [!question]- Why is there no 10-Q for the fourth quarter?
> The fourth quarter is covered by the annual report, the 10-K.

> [!question]- What does a Form 4 show?
> A director or executive buying or selling shares in their own company.
