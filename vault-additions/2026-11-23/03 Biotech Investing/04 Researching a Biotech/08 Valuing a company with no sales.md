# Valuing a company with no sales

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

- A biotech with no sales has no useful P/E. The question becomes: **what is the market paying for the pipeline, and is that reasonable if the drug works?**

## Step 1: what the market pays for the science
- [[Market capitalisation]] = share price × shares outstanding (shares are on the 10-Q cover page)
- [[Enterprise value]] = market cap − cash + debt. This is what the market pays for the drugs themselves
- If enterprise value is near zero or negative, the market thinks most of the cash will be spent on failure. That can be a bargain or a trap

## Step 2: what the drug could be worth
A rough check, with made-up numbers for a company called Example Bio:
1. [[Peak sales]] if approved: patients treated × price per year. 20,000 patients × $100,000 = $2bn a year
2. Value if it reaches peak sales: drug companies are often valued at about 3 to 5 × peak sales, so about $6bn to $10bn
3. Adjust for the odds: in Phase 3 about 50%, so $3bn to $5bn
4. Compare with the enterprise value. If Example Bio's enterprise value is $1bn, the market is pricing in much less than that, which is room for upside. If it is $6bn, success is already priced in

- This is a rough version of [[rNPV]] (risk-adjusted net present value), which professionals do properly year by year with discounting
- The point is not precision: it is to spot when the price already assumes everything goes right

## Watch out for
- Dilution: future share sales reduce your slice, so the value per share is lower than the company value suggests
- Royalties and partners: if a partner takes half of sales, halve the value

## Questions
> [!question]- What does enterprise value tell you for a pre-revenue biotech?
> What the market is paying for the drugs and science, after taking away the cash it holds.

> [!question]- Peak sales $1bn, valued at 4 × peak sales, Phase 2 (15% odds). What is the risk-adjusted value?
> 1 × 4 = $4bn if it succeeds; × 0.15 = about $0.6bn.

> [!question]- If enterprise value is already above the risk-adjusted value, what does it mean?
> The price already assumes success or more; there is little room left for gains if the drug works as expected.
