# Reading a 10-Q line by line

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Researching a Biotech]]

## The order of a 10-Q
| Part | What it holds | Read? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover page | Company name, period, **shares outstanding** on a recent date | Yes, for the share count |
| Part I, Item 1: Financial statements | Balance sheet, statement of operations, cash flow statement, then numbered notes | Yes, the core |
| Note 1 (and the liquidity note) | What the company does, and whether money lasts 12 months; any [[Going concern]] warning | Yes |
| Part I, Item 2: [[MD&A]] | Management's discussion: why costs changed, programme updates, liquidity and runway | Yes |
| Part I, Items 3 and 4 | Market risk, internal controls | Skim |
| Part II, Item 1A: Risk factors | New or changed risks since the last report | Skim for anything new |
| Part II, Items 2, 5 and 6 | Share sales, other information, exhibits | Exhibits list any new deals |

## [[Balance sheet]]: what it owns and owes on one date
| Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Cash and cash equivalents | Money in the bank and very short-term holdings |
| [[Marketable securities]] (current and non-current) | Bonds and similar investments that can be sold for cash; count them with cash |
| Total current assets | Everything that turns into cash within a year |
| Accounts payable, accrued expenses | Bills owed, mostly to trial sites and contract research organisations |
| Total liabilities | Everything owed, including leases and any debt |
| [[Accumulated deficit]] | All losses added up since the company started; large in biotech and normal |
| Total stockholders' equity | Assets minus liabilities: what belongs to shareholders on paper |

## [[Income statement]] (statement of operations): this period's results
| Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue / [[Collaboration revenue]] | Product sales, or money from partners (upfront and [[Milestone payment]]s). Partner money is lumpy, not a sign of sales |
| Research and development (R&D) | Trials, manufacturing, scientists. Rises sharply when Phase 3 starts |
| General and administrative (G&A) | Running the company: staff, legal, offices |
| Loss from operations | Revenue minus R&D and G&A |
| Interest income | Earned on the cash pile; meaningful when cash is large |
| [[Net loss]] | The bottom line for the period |
| Net loss per share, weighted-average shares | Net loss ÷ average shares in the period; the share count rising means dilution |

## [[Cash flow statement]]: where the cash actually went
| Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Net cash used in operating activities | The real cash cost of running the company: use this for [[Cash burn]]. It starts from net loss and adds back non-cash items such as [[Share-based compensation]] |
| Net cash from investing activities | Buying or selling marketable securities, and equipment. Moving cash into bonds is not spending it |
| Net cash from financing activities | New shares sold, loans taken or repaid. Large positive numbers mean money was raised |

## Numbers on the cover and in the notes
- [[Shares outstanding]]: on the cover page. Price × shares = market cap
- Subsequent events (the last note): anything that happened after the quarter ended, such as a share sale or a new deal
- Licence and collaboration notes: what is owed to or received from partners, including [[Royalty]] rates

## Comparing quarters
- Every statement shows this period next to the same period last year. The useful questions: is R&D rising because a big trial started? Did the share count jump? Did cash fall faster than the burn (money spent on something else)?

## Questions
> [!question]- Why count marketable securities with cash?
> They are investments that can be sold quickly, so they are part of the money available to fund the company.

> [!question]- Net cash from investing activities is large and negative. Has the company spent the money?
> Not necessarily: buying marketable securities moves cash into investments, which still count as available money.

> [!question]- Why is a large accumulated deficit normal in biotech?
> It is all losses added up since the start; most biotechs spend for years before any drug sells.

> [!question]- Where in a 10-Q is the shares outstanding figure?
> On the cover page.
