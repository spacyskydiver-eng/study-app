# Vertex Pharmaceuticals (VRTX)

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Cystic Fibrosis Pipeline]]

- **Listing**: Nasdaq, VRTX. **Large cap**, so CF news moves it only a little
- Makes almost all approved CF modulators

## Q3 2026 (quarter to 30 September 2026)
| | |
| --- | --- |
| Total revenue | **$3.44 billion** (up 12%) |
| Trikafta/Kaftrio | $2.44 billion (falling as patients switch) |
| **Alyftrek** | **$706 million** (up from $247 million a year earlier) |
| 2026 revenue guidance | $13.15 to 13.25 billion |
| Cash | $9.8 billion |

- **Switching patients to Alyftrek** matters because it carries lower royalty costs and longer patent life than Trikafta
- **CF pipeline**: VX-828 (next-generation modulator) showed sweat chloride reduction in Phase 1; VX-581 and VX-272 in first-in-human studies. **VX-522** (inhaled mRNA with Moderna) was **stopped** in 2026 for tolerability
- **Beyond CF**: Casgevy (CRISPR gene editing for sickle cell and beta-thalassaemia), Journavx (suzetrigine, non-opioid pain), povetacicept (kidney disease, FDA decision due **30 November 2026**), inaxaplin (kidney disease, data early 2027)

## Investing view
- A quality large company, not a "binary" satellite play. CF now funds bets in other diseases
- Biggest CF risk: a genetic therapy that cures people would one day shrink the modulator market

## Questions
> [!question]- Roughly how much of Vertex’s Q3 2026 revenue came from CF?
> About $3.27 billion of $3.44 billion, around 95%.

> [!question]- Why does Vertex want CF patients to switch from Trikafta to Alyftrek?
> Alyftrek has lower royalty costs and longer patent protection, so it keeps CF revenue for longer.

> [!question]- What happened to VX-522?
> Vertex and Moderna’s inhaled mRNA therapy was stopped in 2026 because of persistent tolerability problems.

> [!question]- Why is Vertex not a typical binary-event biotech stock?
> It is a large, profitable company; one trial is a small part of its value, so results move the price far less.
