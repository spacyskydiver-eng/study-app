# How CF drugs work

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Cystic Fibrosis Pipeline]]

Builds on [[03 CFTR and how it is activated|CFTR and how it is activated]], [[04 Cystic fibrosis pathogenesis|CF pathogenesis]] and [[08 Ivacaftor, a bench to bedside case study|the ivacaftor case study]].

## Two strategies
| Strategy | How | Who it helps | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **[[CFTR modulator|CFTR modulators]]** | small molecules that fix a faulty CFTR protein | people who make some CFTR protein (about 90% have at least one [[F508del]]) | [[Ivacaftor]], Trikafta, Alyftrek |
| **Genetic therapies** | deliver a working CFTR gene or mRNA to airway cells | everyone, including people with **[[Nonsense mutation|nonsense (class I)]]** mutations who make no protein (about 10%) | 4D-710, KB407, ARCT-032 |

## Modulator types
- **[[CFTR corrector|Correctors]]** help misfolded CFTR (F508del) fold and reach the membrane (e.g. elexacaftor, tezacaftor, vanzacaftor)
- **[[CFTR potentiator|Potentiators]]** hold the channel open (ivacaftor, deutivacaftor)
- **[[NBD1 stabiliser|NBD1 stabilisers]]** (experimental) stabilise the NBD1 domain where F508del sits

## Approved Vertex modulators
| Drug | Contents | First FDA approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kalydeco | ivacaftor | 2012 (G551D) |
| Trikafta (Kaftrio in UK/EU) | elexacaftor + tezacaftor + ivacaftor | 2019 |
| **[[Alyftrek]]** | vanzacaftor + tezacaftor + deutivacaftor, once daily | December 2024 |

## Genetic therapy delivery
- **[[AAV vector]]** (adeno-associated virus): 4DMT's 4D-710, inhaled
- **HSV-1 vector** (herpes simplex): Krystal's KB407, nebulised
- **[[Lipid nanoparticle]] mRNA**: Arcturus's ARCT-032; Vertex/Moderna VX-522 (stopped)
- **Lentiviral vector**: Boehringer's BI 3720931 (stopped after Phase 1/2 data)
- **[[Prime editing]]**: correct the mutation in place (Prime Medicine, preclinical)
- The hard parts: getting through thick mucus, the immune response to the vector, and redosing (airway cells turn over)

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the difference between CFTR modulators and genetic therapies?
> Modulators are small molecules that fix a faulty CFTR protein; genetic therapies deliver a working CFTR gene or mRNA, so they could help people who make no protein.

> [!question]- Why can’t modulators help people with class I (nonsense) CFTR mutations?
> They make no CFTR protein, so there is nothing for a modulator to correct or open.

> [!question]- What does a corrector do compared with a potentiator?
> A corrector helps misfolded CFTR fold and reach the membrane; a potentiator increases the opening of channels already at the membrane.

> [!question]- What is in Alyftrek and how does it differ from Trikafta?
> Vanzacaftor + tezacaftor + deutivacaftor, taken once daily; Trikafta is elexacaftor + tezacaftor + ivacaftor.

> [!question]- Name three vectors used to deliver CFTR genes or mRNA to the lungs.
> AAV (4D-710), HSV-1 (KB407), lipid nanoparticle mRNA (ARCT-032); also lentivirus (BI 3720931, stopped).

> [!question]- Why is redosing important for inhaled gene therapy in CF?
> Airway cells turn over, so expression fades; the therapy must be given again without the immune system blocking it.
