# How money is actually made

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## The core does the heavy lifting
- A global index fund has historically returned roughly 6 to 8% a year on average over long periods, with bad years along the way (not guaranteed)
- **Compounding**: returns earn returns. £100 a month at 7% a year grows to about £17,000 after 10 years, of which £12,000 is your money; after 30 years about £120,000, of which £36,000 is yours
- Time and regular saving matter far more than clever picks

## How the satellite can make money
- Biotech returns are **skewed**: most small positions lose some or all of their value; a few multiply several times. Over many positions, one big winner can pay for many losers
- That only works if **no single loss is big**. Typical rules:
  - each position about 2 to 5% of the whole portfolio, or a fixed amount like £200 ([[Position sizing]])
  - 5 to 10 positions over time rather than one big bet
  - never add money to a position just because it fell ("[[Averaging down]]") unless the reason you bought is still true
- **Sell when the reason is broken, not when the price wobbles**: e.g. the trial failed, the cash ran out, a better drug arrived. Price swings alone are normal in biotech

## Costs and tax eat returns
- FX fee 0.15% on every buy and sell of a US share on Trading 212, plus the spread. Ten round trips a year at 0.3% plus spreads is several percent a year, gone
- Inside a Stocks and Shares ISA, gains are tax free. Outside, gains above the annual allowance are taxed ([[Capital gains tax]]); see [[06 UK tax wrappers|UK tax wrappers]]

## Measure against a [[Benchmark]]
- The only honest test of stock picking: did it beat simply holding the index fund over the same period?
- Track it from day one, practice account included. If after a year or two the picks trail the index, more money belongs in the core

## Realistic expectations
- Doubling money quickly is rare and usually luck. A good outcome for the satellite is beating the index over several years while learning a lot
- The biggest wins in biotech usually come from holding a well-understood company for years through approval and sales growth, not from trading around single days

## Questions
> [!question]- Why does the index fund core do most of the wealth building?
> It compounds at roughly the market return with low cost and low effort, and regular saving over time matters more than picks.

> [!question]- What does "skewed returns" mean for biotech satellite positions?
> Most positions lose some or all of their value while a few multiply, so many small positions are needed and no single loss can be large.

> [!question]- When should a satellite position be sold?
> When the reason for owning it is broken (trial failed, cash ran out, a better competitor), not because of normal price swings.

> [!question]- What is the honest test of stock picking?
> Whether it beat simply holding the index fund over the same period.

> [!question]- Roughly what does £100 a month at 7% a year grow to after 10 years?
> About £17,000, of which £12,000 is money paid in.
