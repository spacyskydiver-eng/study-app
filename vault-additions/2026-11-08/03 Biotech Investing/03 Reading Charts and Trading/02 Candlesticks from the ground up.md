# Candlesticks from the ground up

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## One candle = four prices
Every [[Candlestick]] summarises one period (a day on the 1D chart) with its **[[OHLC]]**:
- **Open**: the first trade of the period
- **High**: the highest price reached
- **Low**: the lowest price reached
- **Close**: the last trade of the period (the most important one: it's where buyers and sellers agreed to leave it)

![[inv_candle_anatomy.svg]]
- **Green (up) candle**: close above open. The body's bottom is the open, the top is the close
- **Red (down) candle**: close below open. The body's top is the open, the bottom is the close
- The colour compares close with *that period's open*, not with yesterday. A red candle can still be a day the share finished up, if it opened with a gap up and drifted lower

## Reading the shape
| Shape | What happened in the period | Rough meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Long green body, small wicks** | opened low, buyers pushed it up all day, closed near the high | strong buying |
| **Long red body, small wicks** | opened high, sold off all day, closed near the low | strong selling |
| **Long upper [[Wick]]** | went up a lot, then fell back before the close | higher prices were rejected |
| **Long lower wick** | fell a lot, then recovered before the close | lower prices were rejected; buyers stepped in |
| **[[Doji]]** (tiny body, wicks either side) | closed about where it opened | indecision; neither side won |
| **Small bodies, small wicks** | not much happened | quiet day, usually low volume |

## Worked example
Readout on a daily candle: **O 4.20 · H 4.28 · L 4.04 · C 4.10**
1. Close (4.10) is below open (4.20), so it's a **red** candle
2. Body runs from 4.20 down to 4.10
3. Upper wick from 4.20 up to 4.28; lower wick from 4.10 down to 4.04
4. Reading: it tried higher early, sold off to 4.04, recovered a little into the close. A mildly weak day, nothing dramatic

## Common patterns (and how much to trust them)
| Pattern | Looks like | Supposed meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hammer** | small body at the top, long lower wick, after a fall | sellers exhausted, possible bounce |
| **Shooting star** | small body at the bottom, long upper wick, after a rise | buyers exhausted, possible drop |
| **Bullish engulfing** | a green body that completely covers the previous red body | buyers took over |
| **Bearish engulfing** | a red body that covers the previous green body | sellers took over |

- On their own, these patterns predict very little; studies of them find small or no edge once costs are counted. They are **descriptions of what just happened**, not forecasts
- For small biotechs, news (trial data, financing) overwhelms any pattern. A perfect hammer means nothing the day before a failed trial readout
- What candles are genuinely good for: seeing **how the market reacted to an event**. A gap down that closes near the high of the day (long lower wick) says buyers are stepping in; a gap down that closes at the low says selling kept going

## Questions
> [!question]- What four prices does a candlestick show?
> Open, high, low and close for that period.

> [!question]- On a green candle, which end of the body is the open?
> The bottom; the close is the top.

> [!question]- What does a long upper wick tell you?
> The price went much higher during the period but fell back before the close: higher prices were rejected.

> [!question]- What is a doji?
> A candle with a tiny body (close about equal to open) and wicks either side: indecision.

> [!question]- Can a red daily candle be a day the share price rose?
> Yes. Colour compares close with that day's open; if it gapped up and then drifted lower, the candle is red but the close can still be above yesterday's close.

> [!question]- Why are candle patterns weak on their own for small biotechs?
> News such as trial data or a share sale moves these prices far more than any pattern, and patterns describe the past rather than predict.
