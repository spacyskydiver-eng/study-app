# How news and events move biotech share prices

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## The one idea: prices move on surprises
- A share price already reflects what investors expect: the news is **[[Priced in]]**. It moves on the **difference** between what happened and what was expected
- So "good news, share falls" is normal: the news was good but not as good as hoped, or something else in the release was bad
- Example: Krystal's Q3 2026 results on 2 November 2026 beat revenue forecasts, yet the shares fell, because the same release pushed KB407 and KB803 timelines into 2027

## What each kind of event usually does
| Event | Typical size of move (small biotech) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[[Topline data]] from a key trial** | ±30 to 90% in a day | a [[Binary event]]: the company's value depends on it |
| **FDA decision ([[PDUFA date]])** | ±10 to 50% (bigger if one-product company) | approval unlocks sales; rejection delays years |
| **Quarterly results** | ±5 to 15% | for clinical-stage companies the cash and timelines matter more than revenue |
| **Delay to a trial or readout** | −5 to −20% | money is worth less later, and delays hint at problems |
| **Share sale (offering)** | −5 to −20% | [[Share dilution]]: more shares, each owning less; usually sold at a discount |
| **Partnership with a big company** | +5 to 30% | outside validation plus cash (Arcturus +8% on its Thermo Fisher deal, 2 July 2026) |
| **Being acquired** | target +30 to 100%+; buyer often slightly down | buyers pay a premium (Vertex offered a 102% premium for Crinetics on 6 July 2026; Vertex fell about 2%) |
| **Joining a stock index** | +2 to 10% | index funds must buy it (Krystal joining the S&P MidCap 400, July 2026) |
| **Layoffs / restructuring** | small; can be positive | lowers cash burn, but usually follows a failure |
| **Conference presentation** | usually small | mostly data already released; occasionally new detail |
| **A new academic paper** | usually none | rarely news to investors; useful to *you* for judging the science |
| **Trial registry change** | none at first | quiet clues: a delayed completion date or a terminated arm can come before any press release |

## Patterns around scheduled events
- **[[Run-up]]**: shares often rise in the weeks before a well-known readout as people position for good news
- **[[Sell the news]]**: after the event, even good results can drop the price as run-up buyers take profit
- **Raise on good news**: companies sell new shares right after good data, while the price is high. 4DMT released positive 4D-150 data and priced a $100M share sale at $10.51 on the same day (6 November 2025). Expect this whenever runway is under about two years
- **Asymmetry**: in single-drug companies, failure tends to cost more (−70 to −90%) than success gains (+30 to +100%), because a failed company is left with only its cash

## Which events matter most for CF companies
1. Trial readouts with **[[Sweat chloride test|sweat chloride]]** or **[[ppFEV1]]** results (see [[08 Reading a trial press release|reading a trial press release]])
2. FDA and EMA decisions, and label expansions (Vertex's April 2026 label expansion to about 95% of US CF patients)
3. Cash and runway in quarterly results (financing risk)
4. Vertex's moves, because Vertex sets the bar every CF competitor has to beat

## Questions
> [!question]- Why can a share fall on good news?
> Prices move on the difference from expectations; if the news was less good than hoped, or the release also contained bad news, the price falls.

> [!question]- What is a run-up?
> A rise in the share price before a well-known event as investors position for good news.

> [!question]- What does "sell the news" mean?
> After an awaited event, the price falls even on good results because those who bought in the run-up take profits.

> [!question]- Why do biotechs often sell new shares right after good data?
> The share price is high, so they can raise more cash for fewer new shares, which limits dilution.

> [!question]- What happened to 4DMT on 6 November 2025?
> It released positive 4D-150 data and priced a $100M share sale at $10.51 the same day.

> [!question]- In a takeover, what usually happens to the target's and the buyer's shares?
> The target jumps towards the offer price (a premium); the buyer often dips slightly.

> [!question]- Why are trial failures usually bigger moves than successes in single-drug biotechs?
> Failure leaves the company worth little more than its cash, while success is already partly expected in the price.
