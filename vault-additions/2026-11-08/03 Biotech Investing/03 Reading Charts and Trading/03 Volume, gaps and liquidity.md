# Volume, gaps and liquidity

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## Volume
- **[[Trading volume]]** is the number of shares traded in a period. The bars along the bottom of the watchlist chart show it (green on up days, red on down days)
- Compare with **[[Average volume]]** (the usual daily amount). Volume 5 to 20 times normal means something important happened: news, results, an index change
- Volume **confirms** a move: a breakout on huge volume means many buyers agreed; the same move on tiny volume can be a few orders in a quiet market

## Gaps
- A **[[Price gap]]** is a jump between one bar's close and the next bar's open, with no trades in between
- Gaps happen when news lands while the market is shut. Example from the watchlist: Sionna on 10 August 2026. The SION-719 trial missed its endpoint, and the shares opened about 90% lower: there was never a moment when you could have sold at the old price
- This is why a **[[Stop-loss order]]** doesn't protect you on binary events: the stop triggers at the gapped price, not your stop level
- **"Gap fill"** (the price drifting back to where it was) is common after small news, but rare after a failed trial, because the company is worth less for good

## Liquidity and the real cost of a trade
- **[[Liquidity]]** is how easily you can trade without moving the price. Vertex trades over a million shares a day; a small biotech may trade a few hundred thousand, or far fewer
- The **[[Bid-ask spread]]**: buyers' best price (bid) and sellers' best price (ask) are never equal. Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, and the gap is a cost. Large companies: a cent or two. Small biotechs: sometimes 1 to 3% of the price
- **[[Slippage]]**: a market order in a thin stock can fill worse than the price you saw. Use a [[Limit order]] on small companies so you set the worst price you'll accept
- Costs on Trading 212 for a US share in a pound account: no commission, but a 0.15% currency (FX) fee when you buy and again when you sell, plus the spread

## Questions
> [!question]- What does unusually high volume on a price move suggest?
> Many traders took part, so the move is more likely to reflect real news or conviction rather than a few orders in a quiet market.

> [!question]- Why didn't a stop-loss protect SION holders on 10 August 2026?
> The news came out while the market was closed and the shares opened about 90% lower; a stop triggers at the next available price, which was already far below it.

> [!question]- What is the bid-ask spread?
> The gap between the best price buyers will pay (bid) and the lowest price sellers will accept (ask); it is a hidden cost of every round trip.

> [!question]- Why use limit orders on small biotech shares?
> They are less liquid with wider spreads, so a market order can fill at a worse price (slippage); a limit order sets the worst price you will accept.

> [!question]- What fee does Trading 212 charge when you buy a US share from a pound account?
> A 0.15% FX (currency conversion) fee, charged again when you sell; there is no commission.
