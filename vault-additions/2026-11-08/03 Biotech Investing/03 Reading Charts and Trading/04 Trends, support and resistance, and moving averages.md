# Trends, support and resistance, and moving averages

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## Trend
- **[[Trend]]**: uptrend = a series of **higher highs and higher lows**; downtrend = lower highs and lower lows; a **range** = bouncing between two levels
- Zoom out before judging: a "crash" on the 1M view can be a small dip in a 1Y uptrend

## Support and resistance
- **[[Support and resistance]]** are price levels where the share has repeatedly turned. Support: a floor where buyers keep appearing. Resistance: a ceiling where sellers keep appearing
- Why they work at all: people remember prices. Holders who bought at $40 and watched it fall often sell when it returns to $40 to "get their money back", which creates resistance there
- A **[[Breakout]]** is a move through one of these levels, ideally on high volume. An old resistance often becomes the new support after a breakout
- The **[[52-week range]]** (shown on each watchlist card) is the simplest version: the year's floor and ceiling

## Moving averages
- A **[[Moving average]]** is the average closing price of the last N bars, redrawn every bar. It smooths out the noise
- The watchlist's **MA** button draws two:
  - **MA50** (blue): the last 50 bars, about 2.5 months on the daily chart; the medium-term trend
  - **MA200** (purple): the last 200 bars, about 10 months on the daily chart; the long-term trend
- Price above a rising MA200 = long-term uptrend; below a falling one = long-term downtrend
- **[[Golden cross]]**: MA50 crosses above MA200 (trend turning up). **[[Death cross]]**: MA50 crosses below MA200 (turning down)
- Averages **lag**: they only move after the price has. After SION's August gap, the MA200 needs months to "catch up", so it says almost nothing useful right after a binary event

## How much to rely on this for biotech
- These tools describe crowd behaviour and work best in large, heavily traded shares with no single big event ahead
- In clinical-stage biotech, the next trial readout decides the price. Use the chart to judge **timing and sentiment** (Is it running up into the event? Is it holding after bad news?), and the science and the cash to judge **value**

## Questions
> [!question]- What defines an uptrend on a chart?
> A series of higher highs and higher lows.

> [!question]- Why can a previous price peak act as resistance?
> People who bought near it and watched it fall often sell when it returns there to break even, so sellers cluster at that level.

> [!question]- What does the MA200 show on a daily chart?
> The average closing price of the last 200 trading days (about 10 months): the long-term trend.

> [!question]- What is a golden cross?
> The 50-day moving average crossing above the 200-day moving average, read as the trend turning upward.

> [!question]- Why are moving averages unhelpful straight after a binary event like a trial failure?
> They lag: they average old prices, so they take weeks or months to reflect a sudden permanent drop.

> [!question]- For a clinical-stage biotech, what decides the share price more than chart patterns?
> The next trial readout and the company's cash position, so the science and finances judge value while the chart only helps with timing and sentiment.
