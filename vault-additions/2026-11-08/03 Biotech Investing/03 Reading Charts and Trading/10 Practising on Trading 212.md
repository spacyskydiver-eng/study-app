# Practising on Trading 212

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## Set-up
- In the Trading 212 app: tap the **☰ menu**, then the switch to **practice** option at the bottom. Switching back to real money works the same way
- Choose **Invest** practice, not **[[CFD]]**. CFDs use borrowed money and let losses grow faster than the amount put in; most retail CFD accounts lose money. Invest is real-share investing, which is what the plan uses
- There is no practice version of the Stocks and Shares ISA; Invest practice behaves the same for learning purposes
- This is [[Paper trading]]: real prices, virtual money
- The practice account starts with a large virtual balance (Trading 212 states a limit of $50,000). Practice with the money you'd actually have: pretend the portfolio is **£1,000**, with **£200** positions. Practising with £50,000 teaches nothing about real position sizes
- A practice account can be reset from Settings if it gets messy

## Rules for the practice portfolio
1. Satellite only: at most 5 positions of about £200, from the watchlist companies or ones studied just as well
2. Every buy needs a written reason ("thesis") and a sell rule before the order is placed
3. [[Limit order|Limit orders]] only, so slippage habits are realistic
4. Track a benchmark: put the equivalent of the other £800 into a global index fund in the same practice account and compare

## Trading journal
A [[Trading journal]] is what turns practice into learning. One row per decision:
| Date | Ticker | Buy, sell or skip | Price | Size | Thesis (one sentence) | Event ahead | Sell rule | Outcome later | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 Nov 2026 | VRTX | skip | | | FDA decision 30 Nov; approval mostly priced in? | PDUFA 30 Nov | | | |

- Write the outcome and lesson 2 to 4 weeks later. Skips are as useful to record as trades

## An 8-week plan
| Week | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Set up the practice account and journal. Read each watchlist company note. No trades |
| 2 | Charts: for each company, read the 1Y daily chart, mark support, resistance and every K marker, and note the price reaction |
| 3 | Pick 2 companies with an event in the next 2 months. Write good/bad thresholds ([[08 Event playbook for biotech shares|Event playbook for biotech shares]]) |
| 4 | First practice positions with limit orders and written sell rules |
| 5 to 7 | Weekly 10-minute routine ([[05 Using the live watchlist chart|Using the live watchlist chart]]); journal every decision |
| 8 | Review: compare with the index benchmark; count rule breaks; list the three biggest lessons |

## What practice can't teach
- Fear and greed: losing virtual money doesn't hurt. Expect to behave worse with real money ([[Loss aversion]], [[FOMO]])
- Perfect fills: practice orders can fill more easily than real ones in thin stocks

## Moving to real money
- When: after at least 8 weeks of journal entries, rules followed almost every time, and an understanding of every loss
- How: in a Stocks and Shares ISA, with the core index fund set up first and the satellite at amounts you could lose completely without it mattering
- Practice results don't need to be great; following the rules matters more than the profit

## Questions
> [!question]- Which Trading 212 practice mode should be used for share investing, and why not the other?
> Invest; CFDs use leverage and most retail CFD accounts lose money.

> [!question]- Why practise with a pretend £1,000 rather than the full virtual balance?
> Realistic sizes teach realistic position sizing and risk; huge virtual sums teach nothing about real decisions.

> [!question]- What must be written down before every practice buy?
> The thesis (reason) and the sell rule.

> [!question]- What goes in a trading journal row?
> Date, ticker, decision, price, size, thesis, event ahead, sell rule, and later the outcome and lesson.

> [!question]- What can't a practice account teach?
> Real fear and greed, and realistic order fills in thin shares.

> [!question]- When is it reasonable to move from practice to real money?
> After at least 8 weeks of journalled decisions, rules followed almost every time and every loss understood; then in an ISA, with small amounts.
