# Do events give you an edge?

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Reading Charts and Trading]]

## Events matter, but knowing about them isn't an edge
- News is read by computers within milliseconds of a press release. By the time it's on any feed, including the watchlist, it's in the price
- So an edge never comes from **knowing** the news. It can only come from **judging it better than the market** or **being more patient than the market**

## Where you can genuinely know more
- **Reading the science**: what effect size matters, whether a trial is designed to succeed, whether the endpoint is meaningful. Example: SION-719 was tested as an add-on on top of Trikafta in 15 people. Showing a further sweat chloride drop on top of the best existing drug, in a tiny trial, was always a high bar
- **Under-covered small companies**: few analysts follow sub-$1 billion biotechs, so prices can be slow to reflect published data
- **Time**: most fund managers are judged quarterly. A private investor can hold for years through boring periods
- What you can't beat: speed, information you're not allowed to have, and specialist biotech funds with medics on staff. Pick fights where the degree helps

## Base rates: how often trials work
| Transition | Rough chance (all diseases, 2011 to 2020 data from BIO) |
| --- | --- |
| Phase 1 → Phase 2 | about 52% |
| Phase 2 → Phase 3 | about 29% |
| Phase 3 → submission | about 58% |
| Submission → approval | about 91% |
| Phase 1 → approval overall | about 8% |

- Rare disease and biomarker-driven programmes (like CF modulators) do better than average; new modalities (gene therapy, inhaled mRNA) are riskier, as VX-522 showed

## Expected value
- **[[Expected value]]** = probability × payoff, summed over outcomes. Example: a share at $10; 35% chance of success taking it to $25, 65% chance of failure taking it to $3
  - 0.35 × 25 + 0.65 × 3 = 8.75 + 1.95 = **$10.70**: only about 7% above the price, for a big risk
- Most binary bets are roughly fairly priced. You need a reason to think the probability is higher than the market thinks

## What the evidence says about trading a lot
- US households that traded most earned about 11.4% a year against 17.9% for the market (Barber and Odean, 1991 to 1996 data): trading costs and bad timing ate the difference
- Of Brazilians who day-traded futures for more than 300 days, 97% lost money (Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, 2019)
- About 9 in 10 professional US large-company funds trail their index over 15 years (S&P SPIVA reports)
- UK brokers must show what share of their **[[CFD]]** clients lose money; it is usually a large majority

## The honest conclusion
- Events move biotech prices a lot, and understanding them is the most useful thing the degree gives you as an investor
- Profit comes from being **right where the market is wrong**, sized so being wrong doesn't hurt much. Most people who try lose to a simple index fund, which is why the core of the plan is one

## Questions
> [!question]- Why is knowing about news quickly not an edge?
> Computers price news within milliseconds of the release, so by the time you read it it is already in the price.

> [!question]- Where can a biomedicine student realistically have an edge?
> Judging the science and trial design better than generalists, in under-covered small companies, and holding longer than professionals can.

> [!question]- Roughly what share of drugs move from Phase 2 to Phase 3?
> About 29% (BIO 2011 to 2020 data).

> [!question]- What is expected value?
> The sum of each outcome's probability times its payoff.

> [!question]- Share at $10, 35% chance of $25, 65% chance of $3. What is the expected value?
> 0.35 × 25 + 0.65 × 3 = $10.70.

> [!question]- What did Barber and Odean find about frequent traders?
> The most active US households earned about 11.4% a year against 17.9% for the market; costs and timing destroyed returns.

> [!question]- Why was SION-719's add-on trial a high bar?
> It had to show a further sweat chloride drop on top of Trikafta, already a highly effective drug, in only 15 people.
