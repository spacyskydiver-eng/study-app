# Reading a trial press release

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

Uses the same skills as the course's data analysis work.

## Checklist
1. **Was the [[Primary endpoint|primary endpoint]] met?** Companies often lead with secondary or subgroup results when the main one failed
2. **How many patients?** 15 patients (Sionna) can only show large effects; small trials have wide uncertainty
3. **Against what?** **[[Placebo-adjusted]]** (difference from placebo) is what matters, not the raw change
4. **The p-value**: under 0.05 is conventionally significant. p = 0.7 means the result is easily explained by chance
5. **Effect size in context**: e.g. Trikafta lowered sweat chloride by about 40 to 45 mmol/L. An add-on giving 1 mmol/L is nothing
6. **Safety**: liver enzymes, rashes, tolerability (VX-522 was stopped for tolerability, not efficacy)
7. **Open-label or blinded?** Open-label results (everyone knows they're on the drug) are weaker evidence
8. **What next, and when?** Phase 3 start date, regulatory meetings, cash to get there

> [!tip] Language to watch for
> "Encouraging trends", "numerical improvement", "in a subset of patients", "evaluating next steps" usually mean the result was not statistically significant.

## Questions
> [!question]- In a trial press release, why does "placebo-adjusted" matter?
> It shows the effect of the drug beyond what happened in the placebo group, removing changes due to chance or the trial itself.

> [!question]- What does p = 0.7 tell you about a result?
> It is not statistically significant: a difference that size is very likely to occur by chance.

> [!question]- Why is an open-label trial weaker evidence than a blinded one?
> Patients and doctors know who is on the drug, which can bias subjective measures and reporting.

> [!question]- What phrases in a press release often hide a failed result?
> "Encouraging trends", "numerical improvement", "in a subset of patients", "evaluating next steps".
