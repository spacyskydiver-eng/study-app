# Orders and risk management

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

## Order types
- **[[Market order]]**: buy or sell now at the best available price. Fast, but the price can slip on small, thinly traded stocks
- **[[Limit order]]**: buy only at or below a set price (or sell at or above). Use it to buy near a [[Support and resistance|support]] level without chasing
- **[[Stop-loss order]]**: automatically sells if the price falls to a set level, to cap a loss and remove emotion

## Why stop-losses fail on binary events
- Trial results usually come out **before the market opens or after it closes**
- Bad news makes the share open far lower: a **[[Price gap|gap]]**. A stop at $8 on a share that opens at $3 sells at about **$3**, not $8
- So for a readout, the real protection is **[[Position sizing|position size]]**, not the stop

## Position sizing
- Satellite wallet of £2,000 with about **£200 per position**: one total loss costs 10% of the satellite and about 2% of the whole portfolio
- Never put the whole satellite in one company or one readout
- Decide the exit (target and stop) **before** buying

## Reading a chart honestly
- Use the **[[52-week range]]** (high and low over the last year) for context
- Check the timescale: a flat-looking daily chart can hide a 70% fall over the year

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the difference between a market order and a limit order?
> A market order fills now at the best available price; a limit order only fills at your set price or better.

> [!question]- Why might a stop-loss at $8 sell at $3 after a failed trial?
> Results usually come out when the market is closed, so the share opens below the stop (a gap) and the stop sells at the first available price.

> [!question]- What protects you on a binary trial readout, if not a stop-loss?
> Position sizing: holding a small enough position that a total loss is survivable.

> [!question]- With a £2,000 satellite and £200 positions, what does one total loss cost?
> 10% of the satellite (about 2% of a £10,000 portfolio).
