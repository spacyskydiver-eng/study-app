# UK tax wrappers

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

## Stocks and Shares ISA
- **£20,000 a year** across **all** ISAs combined (the existing Foresters ISA counts towards it)
- Gains and dividends are **tax-free forever**, and nothing goes on a tax return
- Since April 2024 more than one Stocks and Shares ISA can be paid into in the same tax year, so a separate trading ISA (e.g. Trading 212) can hold the satellite, still within the £20,000
- Losses inside an ISA can't be set against gains elsewhere

## Outside an ISA (general investment account)
- **[[Capital gains tax]]**: the first **£3,000** of gains a year is tax-free; above that 18% (basic rate) or 24% (higher rate)
- **Dividend allowance**: £500 a year tax-free
- Big biotech wins are exactly the gains to keep inside the ISA

## [[SIPP]] (self-invested personal pension)
- The government adds 20% tax relief (more for higher-rate taxpayers)
- Relief only on contributions up to **100% of UK earnings** for the year, or **£3,600 gross** if earnings are low or zero; scholarships and loans are not earnings
- Locked until age 57; best once earning a salary

## US shares
- Most biotechs are listed in the US (Nasdaq)
- A **W-8BEN** form (done by the platform) cuts US tax on dividends to 15%
- Platforms usually charge a currency conversion fee (e.g. 0.15%) each way

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the annual ISA allowance and what does it cover?
> £20,000 a year across all ISAs combined.

> [!question]- Why hold satellite biotech shares inside an ISA?
> Any large gains and dividends are tax-free; outside an ISA gains above £3,000 a year are taxed.

> [!question]- What limits SIPP tax relief for a student?
> Relief applies only up to 100% of UK earnings, or £3,600 gross if earnings are low; scholarships and loans are not earnings. The money is locked until 57.

> [!question]- What is the capital gains tax-free allowance outside an ISA?
> £3,000 a year.
