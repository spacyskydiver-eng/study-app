# How a drug gets to market

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

Links to [[05 The drug discovery pipeline|the drug discovery pipeline]] in Seminar 1.

## Clinical trial phases
| Stage | Who | Main question | Typical size | Chance of reaching approval (rough) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Preclinical** | cells, organoids, animals | does it work in models, is it safe enough to test | n/a | low |
| **[[Phase 1 trial|Phase 1]]** | healthy volunteers or a few patients | is it safe, what dose | 20 to 100 | about 10% |
| **[[Phase 2 trial|Phase 2]]** | patients | does it show activity (a biomarker such as [[Sweat chloride test|sweat chloride]]) | 50 to 300 | about 15 to 30% |
| **[[Phase 3 trial|Phase 3]]** | many patients | does it improve a clinical outcome against placebo or standard care | hundreds to thousands | about 50 to 60% |
| **Approval** | FDA (US), EMA (EU), MHRA (UK) | is benefit greater than risk | | |

- Rare diseases like CF have smaller trials, so these numbers vary
- A **[[Registrational trial]]** is one designed to support approval (often Phase 3, sometimes a Phase 2 in rare diseases)

## Endpoints
- The **[[Primary endpoint]]** is the one result the trial is designed to test. "Met the primary endpoint" is the headline the market reacts to
- CF endpoints:
  - **[[Sweat chloride test|Sweat chloride]]**: direct read-out of CFTR function, used in early trials (normal under 30 mmol/L; CF over 60)
  - **[[ppFEV1]]**: lung function, the main efficacy endpoint in later trials
  - pulmonary exacerbations and quality of life (CFQ-R respiratory domain)

## Regulators and dates
- **[[PDUFA date]]**: the date the FDA has committed to decide on a US application. A known, scheduled catalyst
- **[[Accelerated approval]]**: earlier approval on a surrogate endpoint for serious diseases with unmet need, with a confirmatory trial afterwards
- **[[Orphan drug designation]]**: for rare diseases; gives tax credits and 7 years of US market exclusivity

## Questions
> [!question]- What does each clinical trial phase mainly test?
> Phase 1: safety and dose; Phase 2: activity in patients (often a biomarker); Phase 3: clinical benefit against placebo or standard care in a large trial.

> [!question]- What is a primary endpoint?
> The single outcome a trial is designed and powered to test; whether it is met is the headline result.

> [!question]- Why is sweat chloride used in early CF trials but ppFEV1 in later ones?
> Sweat chloride is a direct, fast read-out of CFTR function (activity); ppFEV1 measures lung function, the clinical benefit regulators need for approval.

> [!question]- What is a PDUFA date?
> The date by which the FDA has committed to decide on a drug application; a scheduled catalyst.

> [!question]- Roughly what fraction of drugs entering Phase 1 reach approval?
> About 10%.
