# Reading a biotech company’s finances

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

Most clinical-stage biotechs have **no product revenue**. Survival depends on cash.

| Measure | Meaning | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cash** | cash plus marketable securities | quarterly results press release |
| **[[Cash burn]]** | cash spent per quarter (roughly the net loss) | results release |
| **[[Cash runway]]** | how long the cash lasts; companies state it, e.g. "into 2029" | results release |
| **[[Enterprise value]]** | market cap − cash (+ debt): what the market values the drugs at | work it out |

## Warning signs
- Runway **under 12 to 18 months**: a share sale ([[Share dilution|dilution]]) is likely soon, often at a discount
- Big layoffs and "evaluating strategic alternatives"
- Enterprise value **near zero or negative**: the market gives the pipeline no value (sometimes an opportunity, often a warning)

## Worked example (figures from company releases, 2026)
- Sionna: about $268 million cash; after 46% layoffs, runway "into the second half of 2029"
- 4D Molecular Therapeutics: about $431 million cash and securities at 30 June 2026; net loss about $73 million a quarter, so roughly 6 quarters of burn without new money
- Krystal Biotech: **profitable** (Q3 2026 net income $63 million from Vyjuvek), $1.2 billion cash, so trial failures don't threaten survival

## Questions
> [!question]- What is cash runway and why does it matter for small biotechs?
> How long current cash will fund operations; with no revenue, a short runway means the company must raise money soon, usually by selling shares.

> [!question]- What is share dilution?
> Issuing new shares, which reduces existing shareholders’ percentage ownership and usually pushes the price down.

> [!question]- What does enterprise value tell you about a biotech?
> Market cap minus net cash: roughly what the market values the drugs and technology at.

> [!question]- 4DMT had about $431 million and loses about $73 million a quarter. Roughly how many quarters is that?
> About 6 quarters (431 ÷ 73 ≈ 5.9).
