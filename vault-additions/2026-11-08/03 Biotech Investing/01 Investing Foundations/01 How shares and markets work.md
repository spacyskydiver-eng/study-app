# How shares and markets work

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

## Shares
- A **share** is a small part-ownership of a company. Its price is whatever buyers and sellers agree on at that moment
- **[[Market capitalisation]]** = share price × number of shares. It is the price tag the market puts on the whole company
  - large cap: over about $10 billion (e.g. Vertex, around $100 billion+)
  - small cap: about $300 million to $2 billion (most clinical-stage biotechs)
- **[[Volatility]]** is how much the price swings. Small biotechs are very volatile because their value rests on a few drugs

## Index funds (the core)
- An **[[Index fund]]** buys every company in an index, e.g. a global fund holding thousands of companies worldwide
- Low fees (often 0.1 to 0.25% a year), no stock picking, and no single company can sink it
- Paying in the same amount every month is **[[Pound-cost averaging]]**: more units are bought when prices are low, fewer when high

## Core and satellite
| Part | Share | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Core** | about 80% | global index fund, monthly, left alone | grows with the world economy; nothing to watch |
| **Satellite** | about 20% | small positions in individual biotechs | where the degree gives an edge; losses here can't sink the plan |

- Most professional stock pickers fail to beat an index fund over 10+ years after fees. The core is what actually builds wealth; the satellite is for learning and the occasional big win

## Questions
> [!question]- What is market capitalisation?
> Share price × number of shares: the value the market puts on the whole company.

> [!question]- Why are small biotech shares more volatile than large companies?
> Their value rests on a few drugs, so one trial result or regulatory decision can change the whole valuation.

> [!question]- What is pound-cost averaging?
> Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, so more units are bought when prices are low and fewer when high, smoothing out the entry price.

> [!question]- Why is the core in an index fund rather than individual shares?
> Low fees, diversification across thousands of companies, and no single failure can sink it; most stock pickers fail to beat the index over the long run.
