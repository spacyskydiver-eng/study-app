# Biotech catalysts and binary events

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

- A **[[Biotech catalyst|catalyst]]** is a scheduled or expected event that can move the share price sharply:
  - **trial readouts**: [[Topline data|topline]] results from Phase 1, 2 or 3
  - **regulatory decisions**: [[PDUFA date|PDUFA dates]], EMA/MHRA opinions
  - **medical conferences**: for CF, the European CF Conference (June) and the North American CF Conference (autumn)
  - **financing**: a share sale ([[Share dilution|dilution]]) often follows good news
- A **binary event** has two outcomes, success or failure, with a big move either way

## Real example: Sionna, August 2026
- SION-719 (an [[NBD1 stabiliser]]) was tested on top of Trikafta in a Phase 2a trial of 15 adults
- Result: placebo-adjusted sweat chloride change of **−1.0 mmol/L** (p = 0.7): no meaningful effect
- Sionna dropped the drug, cut 46% of staff and pivoted to its next combination
- Lesson: a strong scientific idea (stabilise the NBD1 domain where [[F508del]] sits) can still fail in patients

## Real example: Vertex and Moderna, May 2026
- VX-522, an inhaled [[mRNA therapy]] for people who make no CFTR, was stopped for persistent tolerability problems
- For Vertex (huge, profitable) this barely moved the share price. For a small company whose only drug it was, it would have been a collapse

## How to handle catalysts
- Know the dates: company presentations, results releases and ClinicalTrials.gov ("primary completion date")
- **"Buy the rumour, sell the news"**: shares often rise before a readout and fall after even good results
- Size the position so a total loss is survivable (see [[Orders and risk management]])

## Questions
> [!question]- What is a biotech catalyst? Give three examples.
> An event that can move a share sharply: trial readouts, regulatory decisions (PDUFA dates), and conference presentations or financings.

> [!question]- Why did Sionna drop SION-719 in August 2026?
> In a Phase 2a trial on top of Trikafta it changed sweat chloride by only −1.0 mmol/L versus placebo (p = 0.7), so it showed no meaningful activity.

> [!question]- Why did stopping VX-522 hardly move Vertex shares?
> Vertex is very large and profitable from its CF modulators; one early programme is a tiny part of its value.

> [!question]- What does "buy the rumour, sell the news" mean?
> Shares often rise in anticipation of an event and fall once it happens, even if the news is good.
