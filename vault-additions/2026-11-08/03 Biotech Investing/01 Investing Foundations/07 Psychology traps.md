# Psychology traps

> [!path] [[Biotech Investing]] › [[Investing Foundations]]

| Trap | What it looks like | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[[FOMO]]** | buying after a share has already jumped 80% on hype | only buy at a price planned in advance; no chasing green days |
| **[[Loss aversion]]** | holding a failed biotech "until it comes back" | if the reason for buying is gone (trial failed), sell; the price paid is irrelevant |
| **Overconfidence** | a few wins, then bigger and bigger positions | position size stays fixed at about £200 |
| **Confirmation bias** | only reading the positive news about a company | write down what would prove the idea wrong before buying |

- A **dead cat bounce** is a small recovery after a crash that doesn't last; it is not a sign the drug works after all
- Keep a short **trade journal**: why bought, target, exit, what happened. It turns trades into lessons

## Questions
> [!question]- What is FOMO in investing, and the rule against it?
> Fear of missing out: buying after a big rise because of hype. Only buy at a pre-planned price.

> [!question]- What is loss aversion?
> Feeling losses more than gains, which leads to holding losing positions too long hoping to break even.

> [!question]- What should you do if the reason you bought a biotech (e.g. a trial) has failed?
> Sell: the investment case is gone, and the price you paid doesn’t matter.
