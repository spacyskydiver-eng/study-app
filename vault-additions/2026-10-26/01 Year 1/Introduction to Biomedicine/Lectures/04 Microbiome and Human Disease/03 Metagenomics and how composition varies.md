# Metagenomics and how composition varies

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 7-9*

- **[[Metagenomics]]**: extract all the DNA from a microbial community and sequence it
  - **[[16S rRNA sequencing|16S sequencing]]**: amplify the 16S rRNA gene to identify who is there
  - **Shotgun sequencing**: fragment and sequence all the DNA, showing who is there and what genes they carry

## Composition differs by site

| Site | Main groups |
| --- | --- |
| Gut | Bacteroidetes, Firmicutes, Actinobacteria, lactobacilli, streptococci, enterobacteria |
| Mouth | Firmicutes, Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes, Actinobacteria, Fusobacteria |
| Skin | Actinobacteria, Bacteroidetes, Cyanobacteria, Firmicutes, Proteobacteria |
| Respiratory | Actinobacteria, Firmicutes, Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes |
| Vagina | lactobacilli |

## Along the gut

| Region | pH | Microbes (cells/ml) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mouth | | 10⁸⁻⁹ |
| Stomach | 1.5 to 5 | 10¹⁻³ |
| Duodenum | 5 to 7 | 10³⁻⁴ |
| Jejunum | 7 to 9 | 10⁴⁻⁵ |
| Ileum | 7 to 8 | 10⁸ |
| Colon | 5 to 7 | 10¹¹ |

- Statistical clustering (PCoA) of samples separates skin, mouth and the different gut regions: each site has its own community

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the difference between 16S and shotgun sequencing?
> 16S amplifies one marker gene to identify which microbes are present; shotgun sequences all the DNA, showing which microbes and which genes.

> [!question]- Where in the gut is microbial biomass highest, and roughly how high?
> The colon, about 10¹¹ cells per ml.

> [!question]- Why does the stomach carry so few microbes?
> Its very low pH (about 1.5 to 5) kills most of them.
