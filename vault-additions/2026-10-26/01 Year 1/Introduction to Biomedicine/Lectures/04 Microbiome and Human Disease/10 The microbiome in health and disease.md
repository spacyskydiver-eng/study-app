# The microbiome in health and disease

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 30-34*

- The microbiome builds in complexity and stability from birth; **perturbations** (genetics, birth route, geography, hygiene, stress, diet, drugs) at any age can tip it towards disease
- A healthy microbiome protects against pathogens, trains the immune system and supplies energy, vitamins and SCFAs. **[[Dysbiosis]]** brings inflammation, oxidative stress, more Gram-negative bacteria, infection and altered metabolites

| Organ | Diseases linked to altered microbiota | Microbiota-mediated changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brain | autism spectrum disorder | bacterial toxins, disrupted fermentation |
| Lung | asthma, cystic fibrosis | reduced immune tolerance, altered gene expression |
| Heart | cardiovascular disease | pro-inflammatory metabolites |
| Pancreas | type 1 and 2 diabetes | reduced insulin sensitivity |
| Liver | non-alcoholic fatty liver disease | altered bile acid metabolism |
| Adipose | metabolic syndrome, obesity | insulin resistance |
| Gut | IBD, IBS, gut infections | dysregulated immunity, altered mucosal barrier |
| Skin | acne, eczema, allergies | pathogenic strains, dysregulated immunity |

## A functional view
- Rather than just **which species** are present, ask **which genes and processes** they carry: **metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, metabolomics** (tools such as HUMAnN map reads to gene families and pathways)
- Known so far: about 25 phyla, ~2,000 genera, ~5,000 species, 316 million genes, ~80% of the metagenome mappable
- Unknowns: ~20% of sequences match no microbial genome; ~40% of genes have no match in functional databases

## Questions
> [!question]- What is dysbiosis?
> A disrupted, altered microbiome, linked to a range of diseases.

> [!question]- Name three diseases linked to an altered microbiome, and the organ affected.
> For example: IBD (gut), asthma (lung), type 2 diabetes (pancreas), eczema (skin), NAFLD (liver), autism (brain).

> [!question]- Why study microbiome function rather than just species?
> Different species can carry out the same processes; what matters for the host is which genes and pathways are active (metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, metabolomics).
