# What the normal flora does for us

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 10-12*

- The microbiome is shaped by **genetics, environment, diet, lifestyle, hormones** and industry, and helps keep each organ healthy, for example:
  - **Nose**: mucus production, antimicrobial chemicals
  - **Mouth**: assist digestion, ward off pathogens
  - **Colon**: digest complex carbohydrates
  - **Vagina**: keep pH low, produce H₂O₂ to kill microbes
  - **Skin**: strengthen the immune system

## Evidence: [[Germ-free mice|germ-free mice]]
Mice raised with no microbes have:
- decreased nutrient absorption
- less developed intestines
- vitamin deficiency
- an underdeveloped immune system
- heightened sensitivity to pathogens

Colonising them switches on host genes, for example **colipase** (fat digestion) and **angiogenin-3** in the gut epithelium.

## Five benefits of the normal flora
1. **Make vitamins**: vitamin K and vitamin B12
2. **Prevent colonisation by pathogens**: competing for attachment sites and nutrients
3. **Antagonise other bacteria**: producing peroxides and **[[Bacteriocin|bacteriocins]]** that kill non-native species
4. **Stimulate tissue development**: intestines, lymphatic tissues, capillary density
5. **Stimulate cross-reactive antibodies**: low levels of antibody against normal flora also react with related pathogens

## Questions
> [!question]- List three defects of germ-free mice.
> Any three of: poor nutrient absorption, underdeveloped intestines, vitamin deficiency, underdeveloped immune system, heightened sensitivity to pathogens.

> [!question]- Which two vitamins does the gut flora make?
> Vitamin K and vitamin B12.

> [!question]- How does the normal flora prevent colonisation by pathogens?
> By competing for attachment sites and essential nutrients, and by producing bacteriocins and peroxides.

> [!question]- What are cross-reactive antibodies, and how do they help?
> Antibodies raised against normal flora that also recognise related pathogens, preventing infection.
