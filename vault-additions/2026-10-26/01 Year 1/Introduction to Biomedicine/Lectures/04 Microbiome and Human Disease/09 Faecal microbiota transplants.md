# Faecal microbiota transplants

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 28-29*

- ***C. difficile*-associated diarrhoea (CDAD)** usually follows antibiotic treatment and lasting disruption of the gut flora; it can be severe, even fatal
- **[[Faecal microbiota transplant|Faecal microbiota transplant]]** (bacteriotherapy) = **assisted colonisation** with a healthy donor's flora
- In the case report:
  - the patient's **diarrhoea subsided within 2 days**
  - their **gut flora resembled the donor's within 14 days**

> [!figure] After a faecal transplant, the patient's gut flora resembles the donor's within 14 days (Lecture 4, slide 29)

## Questions
> [!question]- What is a faecal microbiota transplant, and what is it used for?
> Transfer of a healthy donor's faecal flora (assisted colonisation), used for recurrent C. difficile diarrhoea.

> [!question]- What happened to the patient after the faecal transplant?
> Diarrhoea subsided within 2 days, and their flora resembled the donor's within 14 days.
