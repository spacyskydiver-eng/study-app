# How we acquire our flora

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 20-24*

1. **From delivery**: babies born **vaginally** have a different gut flora from those born by **C-section**. The vaginal community of pregnant women includes bacteria that digest milk (*Lactobacillus*)
2. **From feeding**: the flora differs between **breast-fed** and **bottle-fed** babies
- Skin, gut, respiratory and urogenital sites keep being colonised through contact with other people
- **Succession**: as the gut environment changes, so does the community. The biggest shift comes with the **switch to solid foods**
- Factors shaping the infant microbiome: the mother's gut, vaginal and skin microbiota, **mode of birth**, **mode of feeding**, gestational age, maternal conditions, and early environment
- The ideal (vaginal birth at term, breastfeeding, healthy mother) gives a healthy infant microbiome. **C-section, preterm birth, formula feeding** or maternal illness (often for good medical reasons) can lead to **[[Dysbiosis|dysbiosis]]**, linked to later immune and metabolic disease

## Questions
> [!question]- Name two early-life factors that shape the gut flora.
> Mode of delivery (vaginal vs C-section) and feeding (breast vs bottle); also gestational age and maternal factors.

> [!question]- When does the infant gut community shift most?
> At the switch to solid foods.
