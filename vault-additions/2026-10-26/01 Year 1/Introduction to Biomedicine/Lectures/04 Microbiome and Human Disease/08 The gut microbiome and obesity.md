# The gut microbiome and obesity

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 25-27*

- **ob/ob mice** are homozygous for a mutation in the **leptin** gene and become obese
- Obese mice carry **more Firmicutes** and **fewer Bacteroidetes** than lean mice (Ley et al., 2006)
- In obese people on a weight-loss diet, the proportion of **Bacteroidetes rose** over 52 weeks towards the lean pattern
- **Faecal transplant experiment** (Turnbaugh et al.): germ-free wild-type mice given microbiota from **ob/ob donors** gained significantly **more body fat** than those given microbiota from lean donors, so the microbiome itself contributes to weight gain

## Questions
> [!question]- How does the gut flora of ob/ob mice differ from lean mice?
> More Firmicutes and fewer Bacteroidetes.

> [!question]- What did the faecal transplant experiment show?
> Germ-free mice given microbiota from obese donors gained more fat than those given lean-donor microbiota, so the microbiome contributes to obesity.

> [!question]- What is the ob/ob mutation?
> Homozygous mutation in the leptin gene.
