# The microbiome and medicine

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 15-17*

- **Antibiotics** kill infectious bacteria but also disrupt the natural flora, causing yeast infections and digestive problems
- **Chemotherapy drugs** and other medicines can be **modified by gut flora**, causing side effects
- ***Clostridioides (Clostridium) difficile***: antibiotics remove competitors, so *C. difficile* colonises the gut, a state of **[[Dysbiosis|dysbiosis]]**. Its toxins **glycosylate and inactivate Rho and Rac GTPases**, disrupting the host cell **cytoskeleton and tight junctions** and destroying the villi
- **Bacteria inside tumours** can cause **chemoresistance**: their enzyme **cytidine deaminase (CDD)** converts **gemcitabine** into an inactive metabolite, so it no longer blocks DNA synthesis

## Questions
> [!question]- How do antibiotics lead to C. difficile infection?
> They kill the competing normal flora (dysbiosis), letting C. difficile colonise the gut.

> [!question]- How do C. difficile toxins damage the gut?
> They glycosylate and inactivate Rho and Rac GTPases, disrupting the cytoskeleton and tight junctions.

> [!question]- How can intratumoural bacteria make cancer cells resistant to gemcitabine?
> Their cytidine deaminase converts gemcitabine into an inactive metabolite.
