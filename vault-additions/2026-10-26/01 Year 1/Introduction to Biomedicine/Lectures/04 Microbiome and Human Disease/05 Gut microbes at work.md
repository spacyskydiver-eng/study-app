# Gut microbes at work

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Microbiome and Human Disease]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Microbiome and Human Disease, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 13-14, 18-19*

## Case studies
- ***Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*** ferments dietary polysaccharides (fibre, fructans, mucus glycans) into **[[Short-chain fatty acids|short-chain fatty acids]]** such as acetate, which the host absorbs for energy, plus H₂, CO₂ and formate
- ***Methanobrevibacter smithii***, a methanogenic archaeon, uses that H₂ and CO₂ to make **methane**, lost as gas
- ***Ruminococcus*** species break down **cellulose**, helping digestion
- ***Helicobacter pylori*** is a helical bacterium in the stomach and upper GI tract; a major cause of **stomach ulcers**, although many carriers never get ulcers

## Seven functions of the gut microbiome
1. Protection against pathogens
2. Synthesis of vitamins (K, B12)
3. Immune system development
4. Promotion of new blood vessels in the intestine (angiogenesis)
5. Promotion of fat storage
6. SCFA production by fermenting dietary fibre
7. Modulating the central nervous system

## The [[Gut-brain axis|microbe-gut-brain axis]]
- **Neural**: microbes influence signalling along the **vagus nerve**
- **Neuroactive compounds**: microbes make or modify **GABA** and serotonin-related compounds (gut serotonin does not cross the blood-brain barrier directly)
- **Immune**: microbial products affect systemic inflammation and microglia
- **Metabolites**: SCFAs such as **butyrate** affect immune regulation, gut barrier integrity and brain-relevant pathways
- Example: feeding stressed mice *Lactobacillus rhamnosus* lowered their stress hormone (corticosterone)

## Questions
> [!question]- What does B. thetaiotaomicron produce from dietary fibre?
> Short-chain fatty acids (such as acetate) for the host, plus H₂, CO₂ and formate.

> [!question]- What does M. smithii do with fermentation by-products?
> It turns H₂ and CO₂ into methane.

> [!question]- Which bacterium causes stomach ulcers?
> Helicobacter pylori.

> [!question]- Give three routes by which gut microbes can affect the brain.
> Any three of: vagus nerve signalling, neuroactive compounds (GABA, serotonin pathways), immune/inflammatory signals, metabolites such as butyrate.
