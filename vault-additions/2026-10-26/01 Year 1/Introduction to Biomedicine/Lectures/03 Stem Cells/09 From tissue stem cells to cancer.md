# From tissue stem cells to cancer

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 19-20*

- Gut stem cells in the crypts can give rise to tumours. Some tumours contain **[[Cancer stem cell|cancer stem cells (CSCs)]]**, for example **[[Lgr5|Lgr5]]⁺** cells, which self-renew and produce the rest of the tumour
- **Conventional therapy** kills the bulk of the tumour, but **surviving CSCs** regrow it, giving **drug resistance and relapse**
- **CSC-oriented therapy** targets the cancer stem cells. The remaining tumour cells cannot self-renew, so the tumour **degenerates** and can be eradicated

> [!figure] Conventional therapy leaves cancer stem cells that regrow the tumour; CSC-oriented therapy removes them so the tumour degenerates (Lecture 3, slide 20)
> ![[ib3_s20.jpg]]

## Questions
> [!question]- Why can a tumour relapse after conventional therapy?
> Surviving cancer stem cells self-renew and regrow the tumour.

> [!question]- What happens to a tumour if its cancer stem cells are destroyed?
> The remaining cells cannot self-renew, so the tumour degenerates.
