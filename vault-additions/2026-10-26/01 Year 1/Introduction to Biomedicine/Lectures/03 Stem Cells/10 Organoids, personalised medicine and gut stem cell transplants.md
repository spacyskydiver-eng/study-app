# Organoids, personalised medicine and gut stem cell transplants

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 21-22*

- **[[Organoid|Organoids]]** are 3D mini-organs grown from stem cells
- **Personalised medicine** with gut organoids:
  1. A patient's biopsy gives normal organoids (**cryptoids**) and tumour organoids (**tumouroids**)
  2. Their **DNA is sequenced** and they are expanded and stored in a **biobank**
  3. They are used for **drug screening** and studies of cell signalling, proliferation and survival
  4. Linking DNA sequence and RNA expression with drug response gives **gene to drug associations**, so treatment can be matched to the patient
- **Intestinal stem cell transplantation** (Clevers lab): organoids grown from gut stem cells have been transplanted into damaged mouse colon, where they engraft and rebuild the lining

## Questions
> [!question]- What are cryptoids and tumouroids?
> Organoids grown from a patient's normal gut tissue and tumour tissue respectively.

> [!question]- How do organoids support personalised medicine?
> Drugs are tested on the patient's own tumour organoids, and sequencing links mutations to drug response.
