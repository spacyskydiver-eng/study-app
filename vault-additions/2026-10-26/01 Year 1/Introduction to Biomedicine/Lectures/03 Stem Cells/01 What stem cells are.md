# What stem cells are

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 2-3*

- A **[[Stem cell|stem cell]]** has two defining properties:
  1. **[[Self-renewal|Self-renewal]]**: it can divide to make more stem cells
  2. **[[Differentiation|Differentiation]]**: it can specialise into other cell types
- The lecture follows stem cells from the embryo to adult tissues (blood, skin, gut), back again to embryonic-like cells (iPS cells), and on to cancer

## How much a stem cell can become (potency)

| Potency | Can make | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[[Totipotent]]** | every cell type, including the placenta | the zygote |
| **[[Pluripotent]]** | every cell type in the body, but not the placenta | inner cell mass, [[Embryonic stem cell|ES cells]], [[Induced pluripotent stem cell|iPS cells]] |
| **[[Multipotent]]** | only the cell types of their own tissue | [[Tissue stem cell|tissue (adult) stem cells]] |

## Landmarks on the timeline
- **1956**: first bone marrow transplant
- **1961**: Till and McCulloch prove stem cells exist in bone marrow
- **1981**: first embryonic stem cells derived from a mouse blastocyst
- **1988**: first cord blood transplant, in a patient with Fanconi anaemia
- **1996**: Dolly the sheep, the first mammal cloned from an adult cell
- **1998**: James Thomson describes the first human ES cells
- **2006 to 2007**: Yamanaka (and Thomson) make iPS cells from mouse, then human, cells

## Questions
> [!question]- What are the two defining properties of a stem cell?
> Self-renewal (making more stem cells) and differentiation (specialising into other cell types).

> [!question]- Put totipotent, pluripotent and multipotent in order of how many cell types they can form.
> Totipotent (everything, including placenta) > pluripotent (all body cells) > multipotent (cells of one tissue).

> [!question]- Which cell is totipotent?
> The zygote (fertilised egg).
