# Induced pluripotent stem cells

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 14*

Three ways to get pluripotent cells that can become any body cell:

1. **[[Embryonic stem cell|ES cells]]**: isolated from the inner cell mass of a blastocyst
2. **[[Somatic cell nuclear transfer|Somatic cell nuclear transfer (cloning)]]**: the nucleus is removed from an egg (oocyte) and a nucleus from a body (somatic) cell is injected in; the egg divides into a blastocyst, and ES cells are isolated from it
3. **[[Induced pluripotent stem cell|Induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells]]**: cells from a patient's skin biopsy are given **reprogramming factors (Oct4, Sox2, c-Myc, Klf4)**, which turn them back into pluripotent cells

- iPS cells come from the patient, so they avoid both **embryo destruction** and **[[Immune rejection|immune rejection]]**
- Uses: **disease modelling** (make the patient's affected cell type in a dish), **drug screening**, and **therapy**

## Questions
> [!question]- Which four reprogramming factors make iPS cells?
> Oct4, Sox2, Klf4 and c-Myc.

> [!question]- Describe somatic cell nuclear transfer.
> Remove the nucleus from an egg, inject a nucleus from a somatic cell, let it divide to a blastocyst, then isolate ES cells.

> [!question]- Give two advantages of iPS cells over ES cells.
> No embryo is destroyed, and cells from the patient avoid immune rejection.

> [!question]- Name three uses of iPS cells.
> Disease modelling, drug screening and therapy.
