# Differentiation and tissue renewal

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 9-12*

- As the embryo develops, cells **[[Differentiation|differentiate]]**: they specialise into the cell types of each tissue and organ
- Adult tissues still need maintaining. Many cells die through **wear and tear, injury or disease**, and must be replaced
- **[[Tissue stem cell|Tissue (adult) stem cells]]** do this. They sit in niches inside a tissue and keep producing new specialised cells for that tissue only (they are [[Multipotent|multipotent]])
- Examples from the lecture:
  - **Blood**: stem cells in the bone marrow make every blood cell type (the basis of bone marrow transplants)
  - **Skin**: stem cells in the basal layer replace the cells shed from the surface
  - **Gut**: stem cells at the base of the intestinal crypts ([[Lgr5|Lgr5]]⁺ cells) renew the lining, which is replaced every few days

## Questions
> [!question]- Why do adult tissues need stem cells?
> To replace cells lost through wear and tear, injury or disease.

> [!question]- Where do the stem cells that renew the gut lining sit?
> At the base of the intestinal crypts (Lgr5⁺ stem cells).
