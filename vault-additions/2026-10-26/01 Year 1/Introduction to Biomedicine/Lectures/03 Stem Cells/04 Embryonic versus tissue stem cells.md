# Embryonic versus tissue stem cells

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 13*

| | [[Embryonic stem cell|Embryonic stem cells]] | [[Tissue stem cell|Tissue stem cells]] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Differentiation** | [[Pluripotent|pluripotent]]: any cell in the body except supporting cells such as the placenta | [[Multipotent|multipotent]]: usually only the cells of their own tissue |
| **Role in the body** | develop the embryo into a whole human | replace cells that die through wear and tear, injury and disease |
| **Sources for research** | unused IVF embryos donated, or embryos made from donated eggs and sperm | bone marrow, muscles, skin; fetus, umbilical cord, placenta, amniotic fluid |
| **Advantages** | self-renew strongly in the lab, giving a constant supply; can make any cell type | taken from the patient, they match genetically, so no [[Immune rejection|immune rejection]]; fewer ethical issues |
| **Disadvantages** | genetically different from patients, so rejection can occur; ethical issues over embryo destruction | only a few cell types; lab conditions for self-renewal only known for a few (skin, cornea); rare and hard to isolate |

## Questions
> [!question]- Give one advantage and one disadvantage of embryonic stem cells for therapy.
> Advantage: pluripotent and self-renew well in the lab. Disadvantage: genetically different from the patient (rejection), and destroying embryos is ethically contested.

> [!question]- Give one advantage and one disadvantage of tissue stem cells for therapy.
> Advantage: can come from the patient, so no rejection and fewer ethical issues. Disadvantage: only a few cell types, rare and hard to grow.

> [!question]- Name two sources of tissue stem cells.
> Any two of: bone marrow, muscle, skin, fetus, umbilical cord, placenta, amniotic fluid.
