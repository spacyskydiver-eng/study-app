# From fertilisation to organogenesis

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 4-8*

| Stage | What happens | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fertilisation** | sperm and egg fuse to form a **zygote**, which is [[Totipotent|totipotent]] | 0 to 24 hours |
| **Cleavage** | the zygote divides into identical cells, forming a ball of about 100 cells as it moves down the fallopian tube | days 1 to 4 |
| **[[Blastocyst]] formation** | an outer layer of cells surrounds a cluster inside, the **[[Inner cell mass|inner cell mass]]** | days 3 to 8 |
| **[[Gastrulation]]** | inner cell mass cells move to form **three [[Germ layers|germ layers]]**; the primitive streak (first sign of the nervous system) appears | week 3 |
| **Organogenesis** | the body organs form; the embryo is called a **fetus** at 8 weeks | weeks 3 to 8 |

- The **inner cell mass is [[Pluripotent|pluripotent]]**: it can form every cell type in the body except supporting tissues such as the placenta (those come from the outer layer). It is the source of **[[Embryonic stem cell|embryonic stem (ES) cells]]**

## What the three germ layers become
- **Ectoderm**: skin, brain and nervous system
- **Mesoderm**: muscle, skeleton and circulatory system
- **Endoderm**: gut lining and many internal organs

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the inner cell mass, and why does it matter for stem cell research?
> The cluster of cells inside the blastocyst (days 3 to 8). It is pluripotent and is where embryonic stem cells come from.

> [!question]- Name the three germ layers and one thing each forms.
> Ectoderm (skin, nervous system), mesoderm (muscle, skeleton, circulatory system), endoderm (gut lining, internal organs).

> [!question]- When does gastrulation happen, and what forms?
> Week 3; the three germ layers form and the primitive streak appears.
