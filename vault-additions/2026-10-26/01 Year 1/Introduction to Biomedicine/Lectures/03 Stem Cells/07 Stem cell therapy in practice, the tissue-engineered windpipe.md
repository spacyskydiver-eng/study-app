# Stem cell therapy in practice, the tissue-engineered windpipe

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[Stem Cells]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, Grand Challenges: Stem cells, Dr Mark Williams (PowerPoint), slides 16-17*

- Stem cell therapy is in use or in trials for the **blood and immune system** (bone marrow transplants for decades), and is being worked on for the **pancreas**, **liver** and other organs
- **Case study, the trachea (windpipe)**:
  1. The patient's airway had been damaged by **tuberculosis**
  2. A **donor windpipe** was stripped of all its cells, leaving only a **connective tissue scaffold**
  3. **Stem cells taken from the patient** were matured into cartilage cells
  4. The scaffold was seeded with the patient's cells in a **bioreactor**, where they migrated and grew
  5. The new windpipe was transplanted into the patient
- Because the living cells were the patient's own, there was **no immune rejection** and no need for immune-suppressing drugs

## Questions
> [!question]- In the windpipe case, what came from the donor and what from the patient?
> The donor gave the trachea, stripped to a connective tissue scaffold; the patient gave the stem cells that regrew the cellular part.

> [!question]- Why did the windpipe patient not need immune-suppressing drugs?
> The cells on the scaffold were the patient's own, so they were not rejected.
