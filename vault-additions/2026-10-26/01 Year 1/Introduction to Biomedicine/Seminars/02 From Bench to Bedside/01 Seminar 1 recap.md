# Seminar 1 recap

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[From Bench to Bedside (Seminar 2)]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, From Bench to Bedside seminar 2, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 2-12*

Seminar 2 opened by recapping [[From Bench to Bedside (Seminar 1)|seminar 1]]:

- **Fluid homeostasis in the airways**: the [[Airway surface liquid|airway surface liquid]] (mucus layer over the periciliary layer) is kept hydrated by [[ENaC]] absorbing Na⁺ and [[CFTR]] secreting Cl⁻ (and HCO₃⁻)
- **CFTR activation** needs phosphorylation of the R domain (by PKA, via cAMP) and ATP binding at NBD1 and NBD2
- **CF pathogenesis**: [[F508del|ΔF508]] misfolds CFTR in the ER, so mucus becomes thick and dehydrated, and the lungs become chronically infected
- **Drug discovery pipeline**: 5,000 to 10,000 compounds (3 to 5 years) → 250 pre-clinical (1 to 2 years, ADMET, PK/PD) → 5 in clinical trials phases I to III (6 to 7 years) → 1 approved drug (1 to 2 years), then post-market surveillance
- **Models**: CFTR ΔF508/ΔF508 **pigs** develop gut and airway disease; **intestinal [[Organoid|organoids]]** from Lgr5⁺ stem cells swell when CFTR works ([[Forskolin-induced swelling|forskolin-induced swelling]])
- **[[Ivacaftor]]**: a [[CFTR potentiator]] that opens [[G551D]] channels, the case study for [[Precision medicine|precision medicine]]

## Questions
> [!question]- How many compounds typically enter drug discovery for one approved drug?
> About 5,000 to 10,000.

> [!question]- What two events activate CFTR?
> Phosphorylation of the R domain and ATP binding at NBD1 and NBD2.
