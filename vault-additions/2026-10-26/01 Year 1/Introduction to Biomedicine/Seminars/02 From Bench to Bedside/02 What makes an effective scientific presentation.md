# What makes an effective scientific presentation

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[From Bench to Bedside (Seminar 2)]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, From Bench to Bedside seminar 2, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 13-14*

Seminar 2's task: break into groups and plan your presentation. You are in **Group 4 (Alex, Shafil, Hayden): Future therapies for cystic fibrosis**, presenting in **seminar 6**.

1. **Structure the talk**: background to the field → why your topic matters or fills a gap → methods and results from **2 to 4 key papers** → conclusion
2. **High contrast, consistent design**: a simple, colour-blind-friendly colour scheme, the same layout throughout, large fonts
3. **Use primary literature**: talk through the experimental details of key papers
4. **Make figures do the work**: enlarge graphs, label axes, remove clutter, point out what to notice (an arrow, a highlighted bar); keep text minimal
5. **Delivery**: speak slowly and clearly, make eye contact

## The four group topics
1. In vitro models of CF (understand CFTR function, test drugs)
2. In vivo models of CF (whole-organism pathology)
3. Case studies of current CFTR therapies (mechanism and trial efficacy)
4. **Future therapies for CF**: emerging approaches not yet in the clinic, such as CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing and gene therapy
