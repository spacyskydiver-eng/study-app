# Primary literature to guide your preparation

> [!path] [[Introduction to Biomedicine]] › [[From Bench to Bedside (Seminar 2)]]

*Source: Introduction to Biomedicine, From Bench to Bedside seminar 2, Dr Sean Tattan (PowerPoint), slides 15-17*

Papers recommended in the seminar:

**Models**
- Grubb and Livraghi-Butrico, *Animal models of cystic fibrosis in the era of highly effective modulator therapies* (compares pig, ferret, sheep, rat, mouse, rabbit, zebrafish, fly and frog models)
- Clancy, *Rapid therapeutic advances in CFTR modulator science*
- Silva et al. (2022), *Advances in preclinical in vitro models for the translation of precision medicine for cystic fibrosis*, J Pers Med

**Future therapies (Group 4)**
- Schwank et al. (2013), *Functional repair of CFTR by CRISPR/Cas9 in intestinal stem cell organoids of cystic fibrosis patients*, Cell Stem Cell: the F508del mutation corrected in patient organoids, which then swell with forskolin like healthy ones
- Davies et al., *Lentiviral gene therapy for cystic fibrosis: a promising approach and first-in-human trial*
- Lee et al., *Gene therapy for cystic fibrosis: new tools for precision medicine*

## Gene therapy routes
- A **vector** (often viral) carries a **healthy CFTR gene** into airway cells
- **Integrating** vectors (such as lentivirus) insert the DNA into the host genome, so it is passed to daughter cells
- **Non-integrating** vectors leave the DNA outside the chromosomes, so expression is lost as cells turn over and doses must be repeated
- Either way the healthy CFTR is transcribed, translated and delivered to the cell surface, restoring Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻ transport

## Questions
> [!question]- What did Schwank et al. (2013) show?
> CRISPR/Cas9 corrected the F508del CFTR mutation in patient intestinal organoids, restoring forskolin-induced swelling.

> [!question]- What is the difference between integrating and non-integrating gene therapy vectors?
> Integrating vectors insert the gene into the genome so it is inherited by daughter cells; non-integrating ones do not, so expression is lost over time and re-dosing is needed.
