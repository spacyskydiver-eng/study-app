# Using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 8-9*

Two uses:

1. **Know pKa, [HA] and [A⁻] → calculate the pH**: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
2. **Know pH and pKa → calculate the fraction of each form**:

> **[A⁻]/[HA] = 10^(pH − pKa)**

   From the ratio you can find the percentage of each form

## When pH = pKa
- [A⁻] = [HA], so log(1) = 0 and **pH = pKa**
- **When the pH equals the pKa, the acid is 50% deprotonated**

## Worked example
Acetic acid (pKa 4.75) at pH 5.75: [A⁻]/[HA] = 10^(1) = 10, so 10 parts acetate to 1 part acid = **91% deprotonated**

## Questions
> [!question]- An acid has pKa 6. At pH 6, what fraction is deprotonated?
> 50%: when pH = pKa, [A⁻] = [HA].

> [!question]- Acetic acid (pKa 4.75) at pH 3.75: what is [A⁻]/[HA]?
> 10^(3.75 − 4.75) = 10⁻¹ = 0.1, so about 9% deprotonated.

> [!question]- Rearrange Henderson-Hasselbalch to give the ratio [A⁻]/[HA].
> [A⁻]/[HA] = 10^(pH − pKa).
