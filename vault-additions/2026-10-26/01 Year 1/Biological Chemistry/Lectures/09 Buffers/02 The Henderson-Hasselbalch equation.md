# The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 7*

Start from Ka for HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻:

1. Ka = [H₃O⁺] × [A⁻]/[HA]
2. Take logs: log Ka = log[H₃O⁺] + log([A⁻]/[HA])
3. Multiply by −1 and use p = −log: pKa = pH − log([A⁻]/[HA])
4. Rearrange:

> **[[Henderson-Hasselbalch equation|pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])]]**

- It gives the pH of a solution from the **[[pKa|pKa]]** and the ratio of **conjugate base to acid**

## Questions
> [!question]- Write the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation.
> pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

> [!question]- Which equilibrium constant is the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation derived from?
> The acid dissociation constant, Ka = [H₃O⁺][A⁻]/[HA].
