# Rules for making a buffer

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 17*

1. Must use an **acid-base conjugate pair**
2. Must use a **weak** acid or base (a strong acid's conjugate base is too weak to accept H⁺ back)
3. With an acid, add the **salt of its conjugate base** (e.g. acetic acid + sodium acetate); with a base, the **salt of its conjugate acid** (e.g. ammonia + ammonium chloride)
4. Both components must be at **sufficiently high concentration**

## Questions
> [!question]- Why can't HCl and NaCl make a buffer?
> HCl is a strong acid; Cl⁻ is too weak a base to take H⁺ back, so there is no equilibrium to absorb added acid or base.

> [!question]- Which salt would you pair with ammonia to make a buffer?
> Ammonium chloride (the salt of its conjugate acid, NH₄⁺).
