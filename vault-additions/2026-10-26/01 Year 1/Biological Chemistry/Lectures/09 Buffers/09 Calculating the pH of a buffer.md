# Calculating the pH of a buffer

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 21-23*

- **The concentration of a buffer = [weak acid] + [salt]**
  - 0.1 M acetate buffer: [acetic acid] + [sodium acetate] = 0.1 M
  - 0.1 M acetic acid + 0.2 M sodium acetate = a **0.3 M** acetate buffer
- For a buffer, write Henderson-Hasselbalch as:

> **pH = pKa + log([base]/[acid])**   (or pH = pKa − log([acid]/[base]))

- **Acid + its salt** (acetic acid + sodium acetate): [acetic acid] = [acid], [sodium acetate] = [base]
- **Base + its salt** (ammonia + ammonium chloride): [ammonium chloride] = [acid], [ammonia] = [base]

## Worked example
0.025 M ammonia + 0.021 M ammonium chloride, pKa(NH₄⁺) = 9.25:

pH = 9.25 + log(0.025/0.021) = 9.25 + 0.08 = **9.33**

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the total concentration of a buffer made from 0.05 M acetic acid and 0.15 M sodium acetate?
> 0.20 M.

> [!question]- In an ammonia / ammonium chloride buffer, which is the "acid" in Henderson-Hasselbalch?
> Ammonium chloride (NH₄⁺).

> [!question]- Find the pH of a buffer of 0.1 M acetic acid and 0.1 M sodium acetate (pKa 4.75).
> pH = 4.75 + log(1) = 4.75.
