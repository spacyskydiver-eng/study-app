# Choosing a buffer

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 20*

- **Buffers only work within 1 pH unit of their pKa**, so choose one whose pKa is close to the pH you need

| Buffer | pKa (25 °C) | Useful range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acetate | 4.76 | 3.6 to 5.6 |
| MES | 6.10 | 5.5 to 6.7 |
| Bis-Tris | 6.50 | 5.8 to 7.2 |
| MOPS | 7.20 | 6.5 to 7.9 |
| Phosphate | 7.20 | 5.8 to 8.0 |
| HEPES | 7.48 | 6.8 to 8.2 |
| Tris | 8.06 | 7.0 to 9.0 |
| Borate | 9.23 | 8.5 to 10.2 |
| CAPS | 10.40 | 9.7 to 11.1 |

- For work at physiological pH (~7.4), HEPES, phosphate or MOPS are typical choices

## Questions
> [!question]- You need a buffer at pH 7.5. Would acetate (pKa 4.76) work? Suggest a better one.
> No: 7.5 is far outside 4.76 ± 1. HEPES (pKa 7.48) or Tris (8.06) would work.

> [!question]- What is the rule of thumb for a buffer's working range?
> Within 1 pH unit of its pKa.
