# Calculating the concentrations for a buffer

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 24-25*

Method:
1. Rearrange Henderson-Hasselbalch to get the ratio: **[base]/[acid] = 10^(pH − pKa)**
2. Use the total: **[base] + [acid] = total buffer concentration**
3. Solve the two equations simultaneously

## Worked example: 1 L of 0.1 M acetate buffer at pH 4.5 (pKa 4.75)
1. [acetate]/[acetic acid] = 10^(4.5 − 4.75) = 10^(−0.25) = **0.562**
2. [acetate] + [acetic acid] = 0.1 M
3. [acetic acid] × 1.562 = 0.1 → **[acetic acid] = 0.0640 M**, **[sodium acetate] = 0.0360 M**

> The slide uses a ratio of 0.575, giving 0.0635 M and 0.0365 M. The exact value of 10⁻⁰·²⁵ is 0.562, giving 0.0640 M and 0.0360 M. Either way it is roughly 64 : 36 acid to salt.

- Check: pH = 4.75 + log(0.0360/0.0640) = 4.75 − 0.25 = 4.50

## Questions
> [!question]- Make 0.1 M acetate buffer at pH 4.75 (pKa 4.75). What are the concentrations?
> Ratio = 10⁰ = 1, so 0.05 M acetic acid and 0.05 M sodium acetate.

> [!question]- Make 1 L of 0.1 M acetate buffer at pH 4.5 (pKa 4.75).
> Ratio 10^(−0.25) = 0.562; acetic acid 0.0640 M, sodium acetate 0.0360 M.

> [!question]- Which two equations do you solve to find buffer component concentrations?
> [base]/[acid] = 10^(pH − pKa) and [base] + [acid] = total concentration.
