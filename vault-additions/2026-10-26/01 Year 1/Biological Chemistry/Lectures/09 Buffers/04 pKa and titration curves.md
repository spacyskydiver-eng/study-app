# pKa and titration curves

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Buffers]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Importance of Buffers, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, slides 10*

- A **[[Titration curve|titration curve]]** plots pH as OH⁻ is added to a weak acid (here acetic acid)
- At the **midpoint (50% titrated)**, [HA] = [A⁻], so log 1 = 0 and **pH = pKa** (4.76 for acetic acid)
- Around the midpoint the curve is **flat**: adding OH⁻ barely changes the pH. This is the **buffering region**, roughly pKa ± 1 (about pH 3.76 to 5.76 for acetic acid)
- **The pH of a buffer is most stable around the pKa of its acid**

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the pH at the midpoint of a weak acid titration?
> pH = pKa, because [HA] = [A⁻].

> [!question]- Over what range does acetic acid (pKa 4.76) buffer?
> Roughly pH 3.76 to 5.76 (pKa ± 1).
