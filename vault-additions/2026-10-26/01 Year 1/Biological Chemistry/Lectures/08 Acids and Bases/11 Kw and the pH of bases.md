# Kw and the pH of bases

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 31-34*

- Water is amphoteric and self-ionises: 2H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻

> **[[Ionic product of water|Kw]] = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ M²** (25 °C)   ·   **pKw = pH + pOH = 14**

- Kw links [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻] in any aqueous solution: know one, calculate the other

## Summary of pH calculations

| | Find | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strong acid** | [H₃O⁺] = [acid] | pH = −log[H₃O⁺] |
| **Weak acid** | Ka = x²/([HA] − x), x = [H₃O⁺] | pH = −log x |
| **Strong base** | [OH⁻] = [base] | pOH = −log[OH⁻], pH = 14 − pOH |
| **Weak base** | Kb = x²/([B] − x), x = [OH⁻] | pOH = −log x, pH = 14 − pOH |

- **Example**: 0.01 M NaOH → [OH⁻] = 10⁻² → pOH 2 → **pH 12**

## Questions
> [!question]- What is Kw at 25 °C?
> 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ M² ([H₃O⁺][OH⁻]); pKw = 14.

> [!question]- Find the pH of 0.001 M NaOH.
> [OH⁻] = 10⁻³, pOH 3, pH 11.

> [!question]- How do you find the pH of a weak base?
> Use Kb and an ICE table to find x = [OH⁻], then pOH = −log x and pH = 14 − pOH.
