# Protonation state and pH

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 35-46*

- Adding **strong acid** pushes HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻ to the **left**: weak acids become **protonated**
- Adding **strong base** removes H₃O⁺ and pulls it to the **right**: weak acids become **deprotonated**
- For a weak base B + H₂O ⇌ BH⁺ + OH⁻: at low pH it is **protonated and charged** (BH⁺); at high pH it is **deprotonated and neutral** (B)

| | Acid (–COOH) | Base (–NH₂) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low pH (below pKa)** | protonated, **neutral** | protonated, **charged** |
| **High pH (above pKa)** | deprotonated, **charged** | deprotonated, **neutral** |

> At low pH [H⁺] is high: **if it can be protonated, it will be**. "Low" and "high" mean below and above the **pKa**.

## Practice (answers in the questions)
- **Ibuprofen** (carboxylic acid, pKa 5.2) at pH 2
- **Ketamine** (amine base, pKa 7.5) at pH 2
- **Benzocaine** (amine base, pKa 2.8) at pH 5
- **Isoleucine** (pKa₁ 2.26 for –COOH, pKa₂ 9.6 for –NH₃⁺) at pH 7

## Questions
> [!question]- Ibuprofen (acid, pKa 5.2) at pH 2: protonated? charged?
> pH < pKa, so protonated and neutral (–COOH).

> [!question]- Ketamine (base, pKa 7.5) at pH 2: protonated? charged?
> pH < pKa, so protonated and charged (–NH₂⁺–).

> [!question]- Benzocaine (base, pKa 2.8) at pH 5: protonated? charged?
> pH > pKa, so deprotonated and neutral (–NH₂).

> [!question]- Isoleucine (pKa 2.26 and 9.6) at pH 7: what form is it in?
> The carboxyl is deprotonated (–COO⁻) and the amino group protonated (–NH₃⁺): a zwitterion, charged groups but no net charge.

> [!question]- An acid is in a solution with pH above its pKa. Is it charged?
> Yes: it is deprotonated (A⁻), so negatively charged.
