# The pH of a strong acid

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 25-27*

- In pure water only about 1 in 550 million water molecules is H₃O⁺ (pH 7)
- **Strong acids dissociate completely**, so:

> **[H₃O⁺] = [acid]**   →   **pH = −log[acid]**

- Strong acids include HI, HBr, HClO₄, **HCl**, HClO₃, **H₂SO₄** (first proton) and **HNO₃** (Ka > 1, pKa < 1); their conjugate bases are ineffective bases
- **Example**: 0.1 M HCl → [H₃O⁺] = 0.1 M = 10⁻¹ M → **pH = 1**

> [!figure] Adding 0.1 M HCl releases 0.1 M H₃O⁺, so the pH falls from 7 to 1 (Lecture 8, slide 26)

## Questions
> [!question]- What is the pH of 0.01 M HCl?
> pH 2 (complete dissociation, [H₃O⁺] = 10⁻² M).

> [!question]- Why can you use pH = −log[acid] for HCl but not for acetic acid?
> HCl dissociates completely; acetic acid only partly, so [H₃O⁺] is much less than [acid].
