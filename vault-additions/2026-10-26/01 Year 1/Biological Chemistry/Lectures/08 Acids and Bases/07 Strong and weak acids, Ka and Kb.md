# Strong and weak acids, Ka and Kb

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 20-22*

- Acid and base dissociation are **equilibria**; acids and bases are **strong** or **weak** depending on how far they dissociate
  - **[[Strong acid|Strong acids]]** dissociate almost completely; **[[Weak acid|weak acids]]** only slightly
  - **Strong bases** readily accept H⁺ from water to form OH⁻; weak bases do not
- Water is in huge excess (55.5 M) and effectively constant, so it is left out of the expression:

> **[[Acid dissociation constant|Ka]] = [H₃O⁺][A⁻] / [HA]**   for HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻
> **Kb = [BH⁺][OH⁻] / [B]**   for B + H₂O ⇌ BH⁺ + OH⁻

| | Ka (or Kb) | Equilibrium lies | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | large (> ~0.1) | to the right | products dominate, H⁺ release (or uptake) favoured |
| Weak | small (< ~0.1) | to the left | reactants dominate, H⁺ release (or uptake) not favoured |

This is the same [[Equilibrium constant|equilibrium constant]] idea from Lecture 7, applied to acids.

## Questions
> [!question]- Write the Ka expression for HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻.
> Ka = [H₃O⁺][A⁻] / [HA].

> [!question]- Why is [H₂O] left out of Ka?
> Water is in vast excess (55.5 M) and its concentration barely changes.

> [!question]- What does a small Ka tell you?
> A weak acid: the equilibrium lies to the left and little H⁺ is released.
