# The pH of a weak acid

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 28-30*

- A weak acid only partly dissociates, so use **Ka and the starting concentration** to find [H₃O⁺] with an **ICE table** (initial, change, equilibrium)

**Example: pH of 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ M acetic acid, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵**

| | CH₃COOH | H₃O⁺ | CH₃COO⁻ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial (M) | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 0 | 0 |
| Change (M) | −x | +x | +x |
| Equilibrium (M) | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ − x | x | x |

1. Ka = x² / (1.5 × 10⁻⁴ − x)
2. Rearrange: **x² + Ka·x − (1.5 × 10⁻⁴)Ka = 0**
3. Quadratic formula, x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a with a = 1, b = Ka, c = −(1.5 × 10⁻⁴)Ka
4. x = 4.4 × 10⁻⁵ (the negative root, −6.2 × 10⁻⁵, is impossible)
5. **pH = −log(4.4 × 10⁻⁵) = 4.36**

- The same ICE method finds equilibrium concentrations for any reaction with a known K

## Questions
> [!question]- Set up the Ka expression for a weak acid of concentration c using an ICE table.
> Ka = x² / (c − x), where x = [H₃O⁺] = [A⁻].

> [!question]- Why do you discard one root of the quadratic?
> It is negative, and a concentration cannot be negative.

> [!question]- Find the pH of 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ M acetic acid (Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵).
> x = 4.4 × 10⁻⁵ M, pH = 4.36.
