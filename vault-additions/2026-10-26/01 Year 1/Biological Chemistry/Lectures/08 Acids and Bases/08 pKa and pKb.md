# pKa and pKb

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 23-24*

- Ka spans a huge range, so it is usually given as a log:

> **[[pKa|pKa]] = −log₁₀Ka**   ·   **pKb = −log₁₀Kb**

- **Lower pKa = stronger acid**
  - Ka > 1 → log Ka positive → **pKa negative**: only very strong acids, e.g. **HCl pKa ≈ −8**
  - Ka < 1 → **pKa positive**: most weak acids, e.g. **acetic acid pKa = 4.75** (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵)

## Questions
> [!question]- Acid X has pKa 3, acid Y pKa 9. Which is stronger?
> X: lower pKa means stronger acid.

> [!question]- Convert Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ to pKa.
> pKa = −log(1.8 × 10⁻⁵) = 4.74 (≈ 4.75).

> [!question]- What sort of acid has a negative pKa?
> A very strong acid (Ka > 1), such as HCl.
