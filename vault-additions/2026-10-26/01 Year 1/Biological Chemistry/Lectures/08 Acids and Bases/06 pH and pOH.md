# pH and pOH

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 16-19*

- The concentration of **H₃O⁺** is what makes a solution acidic, and it spans a huge range, so a log scale is used:

> **[[pH|pH]] = −log₁₀[H₃O⁺]**  ·  **pOH = −log₁₀[OH⁻]**  ·  **pH + pOH = 14** (at 25 °C)

- A change of **1 pH unit = a 10-fold change in [H₃O⁺]**
- pH < 7 acidic · pH 7 neutral · pH > 7 basic

| Solution | pH |
| --- | --- |
| Stomach acid | 1 |
| Lemon juice | 2 |
| Vinegar, cola | 3 |
| Black coffee | 5 |
| Distilled water | 7 |
| Human blood | ~7.4 |
| Baking soda | 9 |
| Household ammonia | 11 |
| Oven cleaner | 13 |

- **Can pH go below 0 or above 14?** Yes. A strong acid above 1 M (such as 10 M HCl) gives a negative pH; a strong base above 1 M gives a pH above 14

## Questions
> [!question]- A solution has [H₃O⁺] = 10⁻⁴ M. What is its pH and pOH?
> pH 4, pOH 10.

> [!question]- How much more acidic is pH 3 than pH 5?
> 100 times (two pH units, 10² in [H₃O⁺]).

> [!question]- Can pH be negative?
> Yes, for strong acids above 1 M, e.g. 10 M HCl has pH −1.
