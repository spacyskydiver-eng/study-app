# Defining acids, Arrhenius

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 1-5*

- The word acid comes from the Latin *acidus*, sour. Taste is no longer used to identify acids (many are poisonous or corrosive), but dilute acids in food still taste sour: vinegar (acetic acid), lemon (citric acid)
- **Arrhenius (1880s)**, defined in terms of H⁺ and OH⁻ in water:
  - an **acid** dissolves in water to produce H⁺: HCl(g) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)
  - a **base** dissolves in water to produce OH⁻: NaOH(s) → Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)
- Not wrong, but **limited to aqueous solution**

## Questions
> [!question]- State the Arrhenius definitions of an acid and a base.
> An acid dissolves in water to produce H⁺; a base dissolves in water to produce OH⁻.

> [!question]- What is the main limitation of the Arrhenius definition?
> It only applies to aqueous solutions.
