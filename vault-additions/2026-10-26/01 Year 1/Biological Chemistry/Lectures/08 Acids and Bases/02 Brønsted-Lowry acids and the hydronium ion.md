# Brønsted-Lowry acids and the hydronium ion

> [!path] [[Biological Chemistry]] › [[Acids and Bases]]

*Source: Biological Chemistry, Chemistry in Aqueous Environment: Acids and Bases, Dr Maria Vardakou (PowerPoint); Crowe and Bradshaw, Chemistry for the Biosciences, ch. 17, slides 6-7, 10*

- **Brønsted and Lowry (1923)**: an **[[Acid|acid]] is a proton DONOR**, a **[[Base|base]] is a proton ACCEPTOR**
  - acid: HA(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ A⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq)
  - base: B(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ BH⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)
  - e.g. acetic acid CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺; methylamine CH₃NH₂ + H⁺ ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺
- A bare **H⁺ is too reactive to exist alone**. It immediately bonds to a lone pair on water, forming the **[[Hydronium ion|hydronium ion]], H₃O⁺**

> [!figure] H⁺ never exists alone: it bonds to water to form the hydronium ion, H₃O⁺ (Lecture 8, slide 7)

## Questions
> [!question]- Define a Brønsted-Lowry acid and base.
> An acid is a proton (H⁺) donor; a base is a proton acceptor.

> [!question]- Why do we write H₃O⁺ rather than H⁺ in water?
> A free H⁺ is too reactive; it immediately bonds to a water molecule to form the hydronium ion.
